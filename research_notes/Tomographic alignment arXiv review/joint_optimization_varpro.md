# Joint optimization of tomographic reconstruction and per-view pose: variable projection, Schur-complement and joint Gauss–Newton solvers (arXiv review for TomoJAX)

Scope note: TomoJAX currently runs 20 warm-started FISTA iterations, then one fixed-volume per-view Gauss–Newton (GN) pose step. That is block-coordinate descent (BCD) / alternating minimisation (AltMin) with inexact blocks. The proposal is a VarPro/Schur-complement step: solve the linearised joint problem min ||A δx + J_pose δp − r||² (plus regularisation) with CGLS/LSQR and keep only δp.

How IDs were checked: every arXiv ID below was fetched from arxiv.org/abs/<id> during this session, and its title and authors matched. Papers with no arXiv version found are marked "no arXiv found" and given a DOI or venue URL. "OLDER" marks pre-2022 foundational work.

Verdict legend: **SUPPORTS** = evidence that the VarPro/Schur joint step should beat the current alternation. **REFINES** = supports the idea but changes how to implement it. **CHALLENGES** = evidence against it or for an alternative.

## Q1. Which papers apply VarPro / Schur-complement / reduced-Hessian methods to tomography with unknown geometry, motion or angles, and what gains over alternating minimisation did they report?

### Takeaway
The closest prior work is van Leeuwen, Maretzke and Batenburg (2017/2018). It is VarPro for 3D per-view rigid alignment in parallel-beam tomography. Its reduced Hessian is exactly the Schur complement, and it proves that the reduced Hessian is no worse conditioned than the joint Hessian. However, it only takes reduced-gradient steps. Even with that, its in-axis "tomographic angle" kept low-frequency errors, which matches TomoJAX's rotation plateau. LAP (Herring, Nagy, Ruthotto) solves the same linearised joint GN system TomoJAX proposes, but eliminates the pose block instead. On motion-correction problems it beat both VarPro and BCD by large factors. The newest work (Okunola, Pasha, Kilmer, Nagy, de Sturler, 2026) finds that VarPro converges faster than AltMin from a good initialisation but oscillates from a poor one.

### Cited Findings

**1705.08678 — "Automatic alignment for three-dimensional tomographic reconstruction", T. van Leeuwen, S. Maretzke, K. J. Batenburg (arXiv 2017; Inverse Problems 34(2):024004, 2018). OLDER, central. Verdict: SUPPORTS, with refinements.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Formulation: joint min over alignment a and volume u of a regularised least-squares objective. The volume is eliminated with VarPro, u(a) = argmin_u f(a,u). Theorem 2.1: the reduced gradient equals ∇_a f(a,u) evaluated at the optimal u. The reduced Hessian is "the Schur complement of (WᵀW + ∇²h) in the full Hessian" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Theorem 2.2: locally around a minimiser, κ(reduced Hessian) ≤ κ(full joint Hessian), by eigenvalue interlacing. The authors argue that brute-force joint optimisation "can be severely ill-conditioned due to differences in sensitivity of u and a" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Inexactness: the gradient ∇_a f(a,u_ε) is used with an inexact reconstruction u_ε satisfying ||∇_u f||≤ε. Theorem 3.1: linear convergence, f(a^{k+1})−f* ≤ (1−µ/L)(f(a^k)−f*) + C²ε_k²/2L. This holds if the reconstruction tolerance ε_k is decreased linearly, e.g. ε_k = r^k with r<1. Note that this rate is only linear because the outer step is a plain gradient step, not GN — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- The paper compares three algorithms: Algorithm 1 (reduced-gradient VarPro, one alignment step per reconstruction), Algorithm 2 (proximal variant for bound constraints on a) and Algorithm 3 (alternating minimisation with N_align inner alignment iterations). The authors state that "convergence rates for [alternating minimisation] have, to the best of our knowledge, not been established" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Test setup: 128³ phantom, 128 parallel-beam projections over [0°,180°], 5 DOF per view (in-plane, nod/pitch and tomographic rotations; lateral and axial shifts) with ~±2° and ~±2 px perturbations. The inner reconstruction is CG on a gradient-Tikhonov functional (α=10³) — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Results after 50 iterations of Algorithm 1: shifts are "fairly well-recovered in all cases". With reconstruction tolerance ε=10⁻¹ the rotation fit "breaks down", and convergence may stall prematurely or even diverge. For ε ≤ 10⁻², "all alignment parameters except for the tomographic angle are recovered reasonably well". The tomographic angle "remains corrupted by low-frequency errors, which may point to an inherent non-uniqueness" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- More inner alignment steps per outer iteration (N_align up to 10, i.e. closer to exact alternation) gave "slightly more optimal results but not … a higher accuracy", and even a slightly larger alignment error. The alignment error "always exhibits a slight increase after an initial phase of convergence", attributed to the low-frequency tomographic-angle errors — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Regularisation matters: "choosing α too low may cause the alignment to stall prematurely". A smoother reconstruction (larger α, roughly 10¹–10⁴ chosen by a misalignment-based heuristic) improves early convergence. The authors suggest continuation from large to small α — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- Electron tomography / ROI data: stopping when the maximum alignment increment fell below 0.05 px took 39 alternating steps in the truncated ROI case and 47 steps in the experimental ET case. A second run with a different reconstruction set-up stopped after 15 steps. The conclusions state that adding nonnegativity to the reconstruction improves alignment, and that convergence is "rather slow" for ROI data — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)

**1705.09992 — "LAP: a Linearize and Project Method for Solving Inverse Problems with Coupled Variables", J. Herring, J. G. Nagy, L. Ruthotto (arXiv 2017; Sampling Theory in Signal and Image Processing 17(2):127–151, 2018). OLDER, central. Verdict: REFINES (same linearised joint GN system, opposite elimination, with bounds).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- LAP linearises the residual. It then solves min ½||J_x δx + J_w δw + r₀||² + α/2||L(x₀+δx)||², which is exactly TomoJAX's proposed joint [A, J_pose] system. Instead of eliminating the image, LAP eliminates the small motion block, δw = −(J_wᵀJ_w)⁻¹J_wᵀ(J_x δx + r₀). It then solves the projected image problem min ||P⊥_{J_w}(J_x δx + r₀)||² + reg with a Krylov method — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- P⊥_{J_w}J_x is a rank-≤p perturbation of J_x, so "a good problem-specific preconditioner for J_x should be a suitable preconditioner for the projected operator". J_wᵀJ_w is Cholesky- or QR-factored once per GN iteration. The authors note that solving the joint linearised system directly with an iterative method "has been observed to converge slowly" — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- On VarPro's limits: with bound constraints on x, or when the inner problem is solved with low accuracy, ∇_xΦ ≠ 0 and the neglected term ∇_w x(w)·∇_xΦ "may considerably degrade the performance of VarPro". LAP instead uses projected GN for element-wise bounds on both blocks — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- 2D super-resolution, 2% noise (averages over 10 runs): LAP+∇h took 15.9 iterations, 66.4 matvecs and 15.9 s, with motion relative error 1.81e-2. VarPro+∇h took 21.4 iterations, 468 matvecs and 54.9 s (1.85e-2). BCD+∇h took 29.9 iterations, 91.7 matvecs and 57.0 s (1.82e-2) — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- MRI motion correction, 5% noise: LAP+HyBR reached image error 3.55e-3 and motion error 1.59e-4 in 76.6 iterations, 318 matvecs and 586 s. VarPro+∇h reached 7.92e-2 / 4.33e-2 with 21,900 matvecs and 15,200 s. BCD+HyBR reached 4.35e-2 / 2.27e-2 in 128.6 iterations. In this test, BCD and VarPro stalled two orders of magnitude above LAP in motion error — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- Conclusion: "while BCD ignores the coupling between the variable blocks when computing updates, LAP takes it into consideration", so LAP "requires a considerably smaller number of iterations, matrix-vector multiplications, and CPU time than BCD to achieve similar accuracy" — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)

**2605.06336 — "Nonlinear RMM-GKS for Large-Scale Dynamic and Streaming Inverse Problems with Uncertain Forward Operators", T. Okunola, M. Pasha, M. E. Kilmer, J. G. Nagy, E. de Sturler (2026). Verdict: REFINES / partly CHALLENGES (VarPro wins only from a good initialisation).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)
- The paper builds both AltMin (image updates by MM-GKS alternating with damped GN parameter steps) and VarPro (image eliminated by an inexact inner MM-GKS solve, then GN on parameters with Armijo line search). It targets fan-beam CT with uncertain angles and photoacoustic tomography with uncertain sensor positions — [arXiv 2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)
- Shepp–Logan CT with a global angular offset p_true = 0.2421° and 1% noise: NL-RMM-GKS (N=1) recovered p* = 0.2419° (parameter relative error 7.36e-4) in 1.87 min. Plain MM-GKS gave 0.2421° (1.49e-4) but a worse image (RRE 0.1805 vs 0.0790). Note this is a single global angle, not per-view poses — [arXiv 2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)
- Robustness test: with offsets 0.1–0.2, "both VarPro and AltMin converge smoothly … VarPro actually converges slightly faster", reaching low RRE by iteration 10. At offset 0.6, "VarPro struggles significantly — its reconstruction error oscillates wildly between 2 and 7" and never stabilises by iteration 20, while AltMin settles. Recommendation: "use VarPro when you have a decent initial guess … AltMin when initialization quality is uncertain" — [arXiv 2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)

**2310.09567 — "Automatic and Computationally Efficient Alignment in Fan- and Cone-beam Tomography", P. Guerrero, S. Bellens, R. Santander, W. Dewulf (2023). Verdict: SUPPORTS (narrowly).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09567)
- For cone-beam CT the paper estimates global detector shift and in-plane detector rotation "based on the variable projection optimization approach", validated on simulated and industrial data with code. These are global calibration parameters, not per-view poses — [arXiv 2310.09567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09567)

**2606.21405 — "Geometry Calibration in Tomography with a Differentiable Ray-Based Model", Y. Haouchat, A. Boquet-Pujadas, S. Kashani, P. Thévenaz, M. Unser (2026). Verdict: CHALLENGES the premise that the solver is the bottleneck (points to discretisation smoothness).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405)
- The paper reconstructs volume and geometry jointly by optimising both together with Adam, using analytic ray-traced gradients with respect to geometry. Each geometric gradient costs about the same as a forward projection (runtime ratio 1.07 ± 0.03) — [arXiv 2606.21405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405)
- "With voxels, the loss landscape is piecewise constant, thus producing zero gradients that trap the optimizer in flat regions." A quadratic B-spline volume basis gives a continuously differentiable landscape. It "reduces the final geometry error ||Θ̂−Θ*|| by 55% compared to voxels (0.021 vs. 0.048)", in detector-element units, for parallel-beam 500³, 900 angles, jitter σ=5 — [arXiv 2606.21405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405)

### Inferences
- TomoJAX's current loop is essentially van Leeuwen's Algorithm 3 with N_align=1 and a GN pose step. The fixed-volume GN step uses J_poseᵀJ_pose as curvature. The correct reduced (VarPro) curvature is the Schur complement J_poseᵀ(I − A(AᵀA+R)⁻¹Aᵀ)J_pose. The difference is largest exactly in pose directions that the volume can partly absorb. This fits the observed pattern: translations, which are well separated from volume changes, converge to 0.001 px, while rotations, which are strongly coupled to volume changes, crawl at 0.97–0.98× per outer iteration.
- With 61 views × 5 DOF = 305 pose parameters, J_poseᵀJ_pose is **block-diagonal with 61 5×5 blocks**, because each view's pose touches only that view's detector rows. This makes LAP's elimination of the pose block exact and almost free. The expensive part becomes a single CGLS/LSQR on the projected volume operator P⊥_J A, and TomoJAX's existing FISTA/CGLS preconditioning should carry over. The alternative — eliminating the volume and forming a dense 305×305 Schur complement — needs either 305 inner solves or a Krylov solve on the full joint system. LAP reports the joint Krylov route converging slowly.
- Van Leeuwen's persistent low-frequency tomographic-angle error suggests that part of TomoJAX's 0.02° parallel-beam plateau may be weak observability (Q3), not solver slowness. A VarPro step would speed up the convergent part but cannot recover modes the data do not constrain.
- Haouchat et al. raise a separate risk. If TomoJAX's projector uses trilinear or nearest interpolation and a central finite-difference Jacobian, the rotation Jacobian may be noisy or biased at the 0.01° level. Rotations of 0.01° move peripheral voxels by only ~0.01–0.03 px for typical volume radii. A Schur step built on an inaccurate J_pose will not cross that floor.

### Gaps
- I found no arXiv papers by Hendriksen, Chung (as tomography-alignment VarPro), Tristán-Vega or Espanol/Pasha that apply VarPro to **per-view 3D rigid tomographic alignment**. Espanol/Pasha papers are on blind deconvolution (Q4). Searches on author names plus "variable projection" returned nothing in tomography alignment.
- Guerrero et al. report accuracy in figures, not in text I could extract. I have no numeric degree or pixel figures from it.
- I found no paper reporting per-view rotation RMSE in degrees for VarPro vs AltMin on 3D parallel-beam or laminography data at the 0.01° level.

## Q2. Bundle adjustment and joint GN methods for cryo-ET, electron tomography, X-ray nanotomography and CT motion correction: how do they structure and precondition the pose–volume coupling?

### Takeaway
Computer-vision bundle-adjustment work gives the clearest theory. VarPro and joint LM/Schur-complement BA are the same algorithm except for how damping is applied: VarPro leaves the eliminated (structure) block undamped. That difference alone moves success rates from about 0–24% to about 94%. Scalable VarPro uses matrix-free Schur operators or power-series approximations of the Schur inverse, and must handle gauge symmetries explicitly. In X-ray and ET practice, most "joint" methods are actually alternating projection-matching with multiresolution (e.g. Odstrčil 2019; Pande 2022, neither on arXiv) or first-order joint optimisation with Adam or gradient descent (INR/cryo-ET, CBCT motion). None of them uses a true Schur-complement GN step.

### Cited Findings

**Hong, Zach, Fitzgibbon — "Revisiting the Variable Projection Method for Separable Nonlinear Least Squares Problems", CVPR 2017, pp. 5939–5947. OLDER. No arXiv found. ### Inferences
- Hong et al.'s result maps directly onto TomoJAX's proposal. Solving the linearised joint [A, J_pose] system with no damping on δx and LM damping only on δp, then keeping δp, is the Kaufman/"Joint+EPI with λ_v=0" VarPro step. Damping the volume block as well, or taking only one FISTA step, moves the method back toward the "Joint" or BCD regime that stalls. The FISTA warm-start re-solve after each pose step plays the role of "embedded point iterations".
- The same per-view block-diagonal structure that BA exploits (camera blocks) applies here. For TomoJAX, the "camera" is the 5×5 per-view block, and the voxels play the role of points. But unlike BA, the volume block AᵀA is not block-diagonal, so the elimination cannot be done exactly. That pushes toward LAP-style elimination of pose, or a matrix-free Schur operator (Papalia et al.) whose inner solves are CGLS on the volume.
- None of the X-ray, ET or cryo-ET joint methods I found uses a second-order Schur step. TomoJAX would be in novel territory for tomography, though it is standard in BA.

### Gaps
- I could not access full texts of Pande 2022, Odstrčil 2019 or Hong 2017's numeric tables beyond Table 1, so their per-view rotation accuracies are unknown here.
- I found no cryo-ET tilt-series paper using a VarPro or Schur-complement joint GN step, as opposed to projection matching, fiducial BA or Adam on an INR.

## Q3. Convergence theory of alternating reconstruction/alignment, why it stalls, and identifiability and gauge freedoms of per-view rigid poses (parallel beam, laminography)

### Takeaway
The only linear-rate guarantee I found is for the VarPro reduced-gradient iteration with linearly decreasing inner tolerances (van Leeuwen 2018). Van Leeuwen also states that no rates had been established for alternating minimisation. Empirically, BCD "degrades convergence for tightly coupled problems" (LAP) and joint optimisation stalls through ill-conditioning (Hong). Several global modes are exact gauges in parallel-beam geometry: mean tomographic angle; sinusoidal in-plane/nod pairs (axis tilt); sinusoidal lateral shift (object translation); mean axial shift; and shift along the beam. Low-frequency tomographic-angle errors are only weakly identifiable even after removing these modes.

### Cited Findings
- **Parallel-beam gauge modes (van Leeuwen et al., Appendix A.2).** With the convention "tomographic rotation, then pitch, then shifts, then in-plane rotation", the following components are "trivial … constant transforms of the entire object" and are projected out — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678):
  - the mean of θ_zx (constant rotation about the tomographic axis);
  - joint sinusoids (θ_xy, θ_yz) = a(sin(θ_zx+b), cos(θ_zx+b)), i.e. a uniform tilt of the tomographic axis;
  - the sinusoidal component of s_x, c·sin(θ_zx+d) (constant translation perpendicular to the axis);
  - the mean of s_y (constant translation along the axis);
  - s_z (along the beam), "redundant in parallel-beam geometry".
- **Weak identifiability of low-frequency tomographic-angle errors.** Even after gauge removal, "the reconstruction of [the tomographic angle] remains corrupted by low-frequency errors, which may point to an inherent non-uniqueness". If misalignment exceeds the angular sampling, "it becomes exceedingly hard to find the correct projection angles as nearby projections are similar" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- **Rates.** Reduced-gradient VarPro converges linearly at rate (1−µ/L) under local strong convexity with inexact reconstruction, provided ε_k decays linearly. For alternating minimisation, "convergence can be guaranteed in case f(a,u) is convex in a as well as u … convergence rates … have … not been established" — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- **Why BCD stalls.** "BCD is decoupled in the sense that while optimizing over one set of variables, we neglect optimization over the other. This degrades convergence for tightly coupled problems" — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992). Joint optimisation has "intrinsic numerical ill-conditioning … prone to 'stalling'" — [Hong et al. CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hong_Revisiting_the_Variable_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)
- **Global identifiability with unknown angles.** For unknown-view tomography, UVTomo-GAN-type analysis shows "unique recovery of the image and the projection distribution up to a rotation and reflection" — [arXiv 2108.09873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09873). 2D unknown-angle-plus-unknown-shift tomography is solved by graph-Laplacian initialisation followed by "three-way alternating minimization" over structure, angles and shifts — [arXiv 2511.22890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22890) (Grampurohit, Mulleti, Rajwade, 2025).
- **Bayesian joint angle/image estimation.** A hybrid Gibbs sampler jointly identifies image and view angles with uncertainty estimates in 2D fan-beam CT — [arXiv 2104.06919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06919) (Uribe, Bardsley, Dong, Hansen, Riis, 2021; OLDER). This is a route to posterior variances showing which angle modes are weakly determined.
- **Gauge and the Schur complement.** Gauge symmetries make the Schur complement rank-deficient and require a pseudoinverse or explicit gauge handling — [arXiv 2512.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07969). Low objective values can coexist with very different geometries when ambiguity modes remain — [arXiv 2608.18028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18028)
- **Laminography.** Laminography tilt angles are "not given exactly since the wedge is manufactured with low angle accuracy", and roll errors arise — [Nikitin et al., arXiv 2401.11101, as summarised in search results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11101). I did not verify the abstract page or retrieve a formal identifiability analysis for laminography.

### Inferences
- **Alternation rate.** For a locally linear-quadratic two-block problem, exact alternation is block Gauss–Seidel. Its asymptotic rate equals cos² of the smallest nontrivial principal angle between the (regularised) range of A and the range of J_pose; this is standard alternating-projection theory, not taken from the papers above. A rate of 0.97–0.98 per outer iteration means cos²≈0.97–0.98, i.e. principal angles of only ~8–10°. Some rotation directions are therefore almost reproducible by a volume perturbation, which is exactly what a Schur/VarPro step corrects. With only 20 inexact FISTA iterations per outer, the effective rate is even worse than exact BCD.
- **Parallel-beam plateau (0.02°).** The plateau, unlike the slowly decreasing anisotropic and laminography cases, is consistent with van Leeuwen's low-frequency tomographic-angle non-identifiability. TomoJAX should check whether its RMSE is computed after projecting out the gauge modes listed above, and after projecting out low-frequency θ_zx modes. If the residual error is concentrated in a few smooth-in-angle modes, a VarPro step will not fix it. The fixes would be a gauge or prior on smooth angle drift, or reporting gated RMSE in a gauge-fixed frame.
- **Laminography (30°).** No paper I found derives laminography gauges. By analogy, a global rigid rotation or translation of the object relative to a tilted rotation axis is a 6-DOF gauge. The sinusoidal-mode structure changes because the beam is not perpendicular to the axis. Shifts along the beam are no longer exactly redundant only in non-parallel geometries; in parallel-beam laminography they still are. Expect weak observability of rotations about the beam direction for thin, flat samples. These are hypotheses to test numerically by computing the eigen-spectrum of the 305×305 Schur complement.
- **Practical diagnostic.** Form the reduced Schur complement S = J_poseᵀ P⊥ J_pose on a small problem, matrix-free with CGLS for each of the 305 columns, which is cheap at this size. Its near-null eigenvectors give the gauge and weak modes. The ratio of eigenvalues of J_poseᵀJ_pose to S along rotation directions predicts the per-iteration alternation rate.

### Gaps
- I found no 2022–2026 paper giving a formal identifiability or Fisher-information analysis of per-view 5-DOF rigid poses in laminography or anisotropic geometries.
- I found no paper giving a proven linear rate constant for alternating reconstruction/alignment in tomography.

## Q4. VarPro with inexact inner solves, positivity constraints and regularisation

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 theory (Español & Jeronimo; Comerso Salzer, Español & Jeronimo; Chen et al.) supports VarPro with LSQR inner solves. It supports regularisation on both linear and nonlinear variables, and Kaufman-type Jacobian approximations, with local convergence kept under explicit stopping rules. Positivity is the weak point. The reduced-gradient formula assumes ∇_x f = 0 at the inner solution, so active nonnegativity bounds bias the reduced step. LAP's projected GN or non-smooth VarPro are the documented workarounds.

### Cited Findings
- **2402.08568 — "Convergence Analysis of a Variable Projection Method for Regularized Separable Nonlinear Inverse Problems", M. I. Español, G. Jeronimo (2024). Verdict: SUPPORTS.** With general-form Tikhonov, LSQR inner solvers give approximate Jacobians. The paper "analyzes the impact of these approximate Jacobians … and introduces stopping criteria to ensure convergence". The LSQR tolerance ε^(k) is computed each outer iteration from an initial ε^(0), and any iterative solver with the same stopping rule may replace LSQR. Demonstrated on blind deconvolution — [arXiv 2402.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08568)
- **2601.05224 — "Variable Projection Methods for Solving Regularized Separable Inverse Problems with Applications to Semi-Blind Image Deblurring", D. B. Comerso Salzer, M. I. Español, G. Jeronimo (2026). Verdict: SUPPORTS / REFINES.** Adds a differentiable regulariser on the nonlinear parameters plus general-form Tikhonov on the linear variables. A quasi-Newton method on the reduced problem gets local convergence with "conditions for superlinear or quadratic convergence". An inexact LSQR variant (iRGenVarPro) provably converges locally "despite inner-solve and Hessian approximations". Parameter regularisation "prevents degenerate … solutions", analogous to gauge or weak-mode priors on poses — [arXiv 2601.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05224)
- **2105.14155 — "An ℓp Variable Projection Method for Large-Scale Separable Nonlinear Inverse Problems", M. Español, M. Pasha (2021; OLDER). Verdict: REFINES.** Majorisation-minimisation turns ℓp/TV/framelet regularisation into a sequence of ℓ2 problems solved by generalised Krylov subspace methods, with GCV choosing the regularisation parameter each iteration. It "enhances the convergence of the parameters that define the forward problem" (blind deconvolution) — [arXiv 2105.14155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14155)
- **1601.05011 — "Non-smooth Variable Projection", T. van Leeuwen, A. Aravkin (2016; OLDER). Verdict: REFINES.** Extends VarPro to non-smooth terms, including indicator and bound constraints, where the projection is solved only inexactly by iterative methods. It gives an inexact adaptive algorithm with complexity analysis — [arXiv 1601.05011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.05011)
- **2402.13865 — "Variable Projection Algorithms: Theoretical Insights and A Novel Approach for Problems with Large Residual", G. Chen, P. Xue, M. Gan, J. Chen, W. Guo, C. L. P. Chen (2024). Verdict: SUPPORTS (the Kaufman shortcut).** Proves that "Kaufman's VP algorithm can achieve a similar convergence rate as the Golub & Pereyra's form". For large-residual problems it proposes VPLR, which corrects the approximate Hessian iteratively — [arXiv 2402.13865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13865)
- **LAP on constraints (1705.09992).** VarPro has "not straightforward" inequality constraints on the linear variables. With bounds, or a low-accuracy inner solve, the neglected term "may considerably degrade the performance of VarPro". LAP imposes element-wise bounds on both blocks by projected GN — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- **Nonnegativity helps alignment.** Van Leeuwen et al. found that "the alignment can be improved by additionally imposing a non-negativity constraint", and that a too-loose inner tolerance (ε=10⁻¹) stalls or diverges rotation estimation — [arXiv 1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)
- **Related VarPro solvers for sparse regularisation.** VPAL (variable projected augmented Lagrangian) handles generalised-lasso/TV problems — [arXiv 2207.08216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08216), Chung & Renaut 2022; [arXiv 2510.24140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24140), Aleotti, Bianchi, Bossmann, Chen, Chung 2025.

### Inferences
- TomoJAX's proposed step (linearised joint LSQR, keep δp) is the Kaufman/Ruhe–Wedin-type VarPro GN step. Chen et al. 2024 support its local rate being comparable to full Golub–Pereyra, so the second-order term can be dropped.
- Positivity: TomoJAX's volume is nonnegative with no support, and FISTA with projection leaves an active set. Inside the linearised joint solve, either:
  - (a) restrict δx to the free (inactive) voxels, as in projected GN / LAP, by masking A's columns to the current inactive set; or
  - (b) keep the unconstrained linearised solve for δp only, then re-solve the volume with FISTA (the "embedded point iteration").

  Option (b) is cheaper and matches Hong's EPI. Option (a) is more faithful to LAP's evidence.
- Inner accuracy: use an Español–Jeronimo-style decreasing LSQR tolerance per outer iteration rather than a fixed iteration count. Van Leeuwen's ε=10⁻¹ failure shows that too-loose inner solves specifically damage rotations.
- Regularisation in the elimination is essential. With free voxels and only ~61 views, A has a large near-null space. Without Tikhonov or TV in the eliminated block, P⊥_A J_pose can be near zero for rotation directions, because the volume absorbs them. The Schur step then becomes ill-posed. This mirrors van Leeuwen's "α too low → stall". Use continuation in α.

### Gaps
- I found no paper analysing VarPro with nonnegativity constraints and inexact FISTA inner solves together (as opposed to LSQR/CG with Tikhonov).

## Q5. Evidence that joint quasi-Newton (L-BFGS over volume and poses), Anderson acceleration or outer-loop momentum matches or beats VarPro

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and indirect. Recent tomography calibration papers (Haouchat 2026; Jiang 2025; Lim 2025; Thies 2024) use first-order joint optimisation (Adam or gradient descent) and report good image-level results. None reports rotation accuracy at the 0.01° level, and none compares against VarPro. Hong 2017 and LAP 2017/18 show that naive joint optimisation stalls on separable problems. Anderson acceleration has evidence only in seismic inversion, not tomographic alignment. Overall the literature favours a Schur/VarPro/LAP-type second-order step over joint L-BFGS or Adam for the final high-accuracy phase. AltMin or first-order joint optimisation remains the robust choice far from the solution.

### Cited Findings
- **Joint first-order calibration with Adam works at image level** (PSNR/SSIM gains, a 55% geometry-error reduction with B-splines vs voxels) but was not compared to VarPro — [arXiv 2606.21405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405)
- **Gradient descent with analytic Jacobians** for CBCT rigid motion: 19× faster than gradient-free methods; reprojection error 3 mm → 0.61 mm — [arXiv 2401.09283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09283). Analytic projector gradients give an ~8× speedup over an existing differentiable projector; optimisation used Adam — [arXiv 2508.13304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13304)
- **Joint optimisation stalls** on separable least squares from arbitrary initialisation (4% success vs 94% for VarPro on affine BA) — [Hong et al. CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hong_Revisiting_the_Variable_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf). Solving the coupled linearised GN system directly with an iterative method "has been observed to converge slowly" — [arXiv 1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)
- **Anderson acceleration.** AA applied to steepest descent as a fixed-point iteration "can achieve fast convergence and provide competitive results with some quasi-Newton methods" for FWI and LSRTM (Marmousi). The paper discusses links to L-BFGS and restarted GMRES — [arXiv 2008.11778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11778) (Y. Yang, 2020; OLDER, seismic not tomography). **Verdict: weak CHALLENGE / alternative.**
- **Quasi-Newton inside VarPro** (on the reduced problem, not jointly) gets superlinear local convergence — [arXiv 2601.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05224). **Verdict: REFINES** — L-BFGS on the reduced pose problem is an alternative to a full Schur GN.
- **AltMin is more robust far from the solution.** It "effectively smooths out this landscape at the cost of requiring more outer iterations" — [arXiv 2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)

### Inferences
- **Cheapest test before building full VarPro.** Treat TomoJAX's outer map (20 FISTA + GN pose) as a fixed-point iteration on the 305 pose parameters and apply Anderson acceleration with m≈5–10, after translations have converged. A contraction factor of 0.97–0.98 with a few slow, smooth modes is the regime where AA or GMRES-like extrapolation tends to help most. However, I found no tomography-alignment evidence for this, and AA cannot fix non-identifiable (plateau) modes.
- **Recommended staging, consistent with Okunola 2026 and Hong 2017.**
  1. Keep the current AltMin for the coarse and translation phase.
  2. Switch to a VarPro/LAP joint GN step once poses are near the solution: undamped volume, LM-damped pose, decreasing LSQR tolerance, nonnegativity via active-set masking, Tikhonov continuation.
  3. Report rotation RMSE after gauge projection (Q3).
- Joint L-BFGS over ~10⁶–10⁸ voxels plus 305 pose parameters inherits the scale mismatch van Leeuwen and Hong describe. Without a diagonal rescaling between blocks it is likely to behave like "Joint" optimisation; I found no tomography evidence either way.

### Gaps
- I found no paper directly comparing joint L-BFGS, Anderson-accelerated alternation, and VarPro/Schur GN on the same tomographic alignment problem.
- I found no paper applying Anderson acceleration or Nesterov momentum to the outer reconstruction/alignment loop in tomography.
