# Faster convergence of iterative tomographic reconstruction for tilted, limited-angle, anisotropic and irregular geometry: preconditioning, Krylov, multilevel, stochastic and initialisation methods (arXiv review for TomoJAX)

Scope and verification note: every arXiv ID below was checked against the arXiv API (`export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=...`) on 2026-10-04 and the title/authors/date match. Quantitative numbers come from the paper PDFs or abstracts, read via pdftotext. Papers from before 2022 are marked **[older]** and are included only when they are the primary source for a technique. The TomoJAX context assumed throughout: a 27-cell frozen matrix with full-volume relative-L2 gates; Joseph-projector CGLS for 30° laminography (plain CGLS needs more than 256 iterations at 256³, while a coarse-to-fine CGLS passes); FBP-type methods for parallel and anisotropic geometry; one sharp, structured, anisotropic, shifted 64³ cell that fails for every method including ASTRA (FBP 0.178 against a 0.15 gate); nonnegative Huber-TV FISTA (20 iterations, warm-started) as the inner volume solve of joint alignment; and a laminography plateau of about 0.12 relative L2 caused by the missing cone.

## Q1. Preconditioning for CGLS/LSQR/FISTA in CT (filtered/ramp/Fourier, diagonal SIRT-type, randomised/polynomial), and how it behaves for laminography and limited angle

### Takeaway
A ramp-filtered backprojector used as the "B" in a Krylov solver is the best-evidenced single change. In 3D cone-beam CT it cut the iterations needed to reach minimum image error from 29 to 4, at roughly the same RMSE, and it beats plain FBP. A Krylov method with an unmatched B (BA-/AB-GMRES) is needed for this, because CGLS breaks when B ≠ Aᵀ. Volume-domain (3D-convolution) preconditioners work when the normal operator's PSF is close to shift-invariant, which holds for parallel-beam full-rotation laminography. No published arXiv study tests a ramp or Fourier preconditioner on laminography specifically. Preconditioning speeds convergence; it cannot recover the missing cone.

### Cited Findings

**Ramp-filter preconditioning through BA-GMRES (most directly actionable)**
- **arXiv:2201.07408**, "Iterative image reconstruction for CT with unmatched projection matrices using the generalized minimal residual algorithm", Emil Y. Sidky, Per Christian Hansen, Jakob S. Jørgensen, Xiaochuan Pan (2022, CT Meeting). Method: BA-GMRES solves BAx = Bb. Here A is a ray-driven (line-intersection) cone-beam projector, and B is either (1) B_un, a voxel-driven linear-interpolation backprojector (unmatched, ≈Aᵀ), or (2) B_FBP = B_un·F, with F the ramp filter, which acts as a preconditioner. Data: real CBCT QA phantom, 180 views (4× subsampled from 720), 1088×896 detector, reconstructed on 1024×1024×300 voxels. Results: CGLS with the unmatched pair starts to diverge in data RMSE after about 20 iterations. BA-GMRES with B_un reaches its minimum image RMSE of 0.0201 at iteration 29. Ramp-preconditioned BA-GMRES reaches a minimum image RMSE of 0.0210 at **iteration 4**. 180-view FBP has an image RMSE of 0.0347. The authors note the "practical dilemma" that with preconditioning, semi-convergence arrives so quickly that "the iteration number provides only coarse control over its image quality". By iteration 20, preconditioned BA-GMRES shows strong over-iteration artefacts. — [arXiv:2201.07408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07408)
- **arXiv:2110.01481** **[older, 2021, foundational]**, "GMRES Methods for Tomographic Reconstruction with an Unmatched Back Projector", Per Christian Hansen, Ken Hayami, Keiichi Morikuni (published in J. Comput. Appl. Math.). Method: AB-GMRES (ABy = b, x = By) and BA-GMRES (BAx = Bb). With a matched pair they are equivalent to LSQR and LSMR respectively. They need only the available A and B, and they have no tuning parameters beyond the iteration count. They show semi-convergence comparable to LSQR/LSMR, and standard stopping rules work. With A matched to the generating model, LSQR/LSMR give slightly smaller errors than AB/BA-GMRES. BA-GMRES uses more iterations but less storage than AB-GMRES when m ≈ 1.4n, because its Krylov vectors live in image space. — [arXiv:2110.01481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01481)
- **arXiv:1902.04282** **[older, 2019]**, "Fixing Nonconvergence of Algebraic Iterative Reconstruction with an Unmatched Backprojector", Yiqiu Dong, Per Christian Hansen, Michiel E. Hochstenbach, Nicolai Andre Brogaard Riis (SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 41, 2019). With B ≠ Aᵀ, SIRT-type iterations can diverge when BA has eigenvalues with negative real part. A small shift parameter, chosen from a Krylov estimate of the leftmost eigenvalue, guarantees convergence to the fixed point of a slightly perturbed problem. — [arXiv:1902.04282](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04282)

**Volume-domain (3D convolution) preconditioner plus multigrid**
- **arXiv:2103.10329** **[older; SPIE conference paper posted 2021]**, "Rapidly-converging multigrid reconstruction of cone-beam tomographic data", Glenn R. Myers, Andrew M. Kingston, Shane J. Latham, Benoit Recur, Thomas Li, Michael L. Turner, Levi Beeching, Adrian P. Sheppard. Method: preconditioned Landweber μ ← μ + p ∗ B(I − Pμ), where p is a 3×3×3 empirically chosen deconvolution kernel applied in volume space, inside a recursive full-multigrid scheme. OS-EMTR runs at the coarsest grid, and each level applies L1 soft-thresholding plus a bilateral filter. The kernel works because the PSF of BP is close to spatially invariant for a space-filling trajectory. Data: 3600 radiographs (subset of 11,099, downsampled 4×), 640×640×2304 voxels, 3 Titan X GPUs. Result: an acceptable image after **1–2 full-resolution iterations**, each costing about one SIRT iteration. 10 iterations of SIRT and 10 of OS-EMTR were still blurred. The authors also scaled the method to a 186-gigavoxel volume in 16.15 h. Caveat: the kernel has zero DC response, so grey levels drift under soft-thresholding, and the authors say "a more sophisticated preconditioning kernel is required". Evidence is visual only, with no error metric. — [arXiv:2103.10329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10329)

**Exploiting Toeplitz structure of AᵀA (for preconditioning and fast gradients)**
- **arXiv:2603.28756**, "Fast Large-Scale Model-Based Iterative Tomography via Exploiting Mathematical Structure, Hierarchical Optimization, Smart Initialization, and Distributed GPU Computing", Dinesh Kumar, Jeffrey Donatelli (2026). For parallel-beam MBIR (qGGMRF/TV prior, Nesterov-type gradient method), they write R*R as a multilevel Toeplitz operator: R*Rf = Res_{N×N}(F⁻¹(FK ⊙ F Pad_{2N−1} f)). Each gradient then costs two FFTs on a 2× padded grid instead of a NUFFT forward/adjoint pair. FK and R*g are precomputed once. They report an "optimized Toeplitz implementation [that] consistently reduces computation time while maintaining high numerical accuracy" (deviation within single precision). — [arXiv:2603.28756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28756)
- **arXiv:2304.12934**, "tomoCAM: Fast Model-based Iterative Reconstruction via GPU Acceleration and Non-Uniform Fast Fourier Transforms", Dinesh Kumar, Dilworth Y. Parkinson, Jeffrey J. Donatelli (2023). This is the predecessor: NUFFT-based Radon and adjoint for GPU MBIR, aimed at sparse-angle and in-situ synchrotron data. — [arXiv:2304.12934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12934)

**Matrix-free generic preconditioners for FISTA-type solvers**
- **arXiv:2411.08178**, "Using Randomized Nyström Preconditioners to Accelerate Variational Image Reconstruction", Tao Hong, Zhaoyi Xu, Jason Hu, Jeffrey A. Fessler (2024; v2 Oct 2025). They build a rank-K randomised Nyström approximation of AᵀA on the fly on GPU, matrix-free, and use it as a preconditioner P. Weighted prox subproblems with P (rank-K plus identity) are solved via a closed-form result (their Theorem 1) for wavelet, TV and Hessian–Schatten regularisers. CT tests: 2D only, 512×512, 100 projections, parallel and fan beam (ODL). K = 20 for wavelet/TV and K = 100 for HS1. Preconditioned WAPG (FISTA-like) converged faster than unpreconditioned in both iterations and wall time, and ADMM was slowest. Memory cost: K extra images, which the authors flag as a limitation for high-dimensional GPU problems. — [arXiv:2411.08178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08178)
- **arXiv:2204.10252**, "Polynomial Preconditioners for Regularized Linear Inverse Problems", Siddharth Srinivasan Iyer, Frank Ong, Xiaozhi Cao, Congyu Liao, Luca Daniel, Jonathan I. Tamir, Kawin Setsompop (2022). A polynomial in AᴴA is chosen to target its eigenvalue spectrum and is used as a preconditioner inside proximal gradient methods. It assumes no explicit operator structure. Validation was on MRI only, with faster convergence at similar quality. — [arXiv:2204.10252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10252)
- **arXiv:2410.09837**, "Tomographic Model Based Iterative Reconstruction of Symmetric Objects", Kyle M. Champley et al. (2024, LEAP/LLNL). MBIR for cylindrically symmetric objects from cone-beam data. Per the search summary, they use a preconditioner approximating the inverse Hessian of the data term to fix slow convergence near the symmetry axis. This is an example of geometry-specific, spatially varying slow modes being fixed by a Hessian-approximation preconditioner. — [arXiv:2410.09837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09837)
- **arXiv:2010.01371** **[older, 2020]**, "A Preconditioned Algorithm for Model-Based Iterative CT Reconstruction and Material Decomposition from Spectral CT Data", Matthew Tivnan, Wenying Wang, J. Webster Stayman. Per search excerpt, the Fourier preconditioner is a linear filter applied as FFT → diagonal scaling → inverse FFT. This is the generic "ramp-like in the image domain" construction. — [arXiv:2010.01371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01371)

### Inferences
- **Laminography CGLS at 256³ (>256 iterations).** Classic CGLS slow-mode behaviour: AᵀA has a ~1/|k| symbol, so high frequencies converge last. For parallel-beam 30° laminography with a full 360° rotation, AᵀA is approximately shift-invariant: a convolution whose Fourier symbol is ~1/|k| outside the missing cone and 0 inside it. Two geometry-general preconditioners therefore follow (my inference, not tested in the cited laminography literature):
  1. **Probe-and-invert Toeplitz preconditioner.** Apply AᵀA once to a centred delta on a 2N-padded grid to get the kernel K. Precompute FK, then precondition with F⁻¹(1/(FK + ε))F, regularised in the missing-cone region. This combines Kumar–Donatelli's Toeplitz trick (2603.28756) with the Myers volume-kernel idea (2103.10329). It needs only the existing Joseph A/Aᵀ, is geometry-agnostic as long as the trajectory is close to shift-invariant, and costs 2 FFTs per iteration on 2N³ (or N³ with circulant approximation). It can also replace A/Aᵀ entirely for the gradient of the quadratic data term, i.e. Toeplitz-embedded CG.
  2. **Ramp-filtered backprojector as B in BA-GMRES**, following Sidky et al. For laminography the filter should be a 2D ramp, or a laminography-FBP filter, on the detector. Expect the 29→4 iteration reduction to transfer qualitatively, but with early semi-convergence, so stopping control matters.
- Because CGLS needs B = Aᵀ, switching to filtered B **requires** a non-symmetric Krylov method (BA-GMRES, or the GKB variants in Q2). Alternatively, apply the preconditioner symmetrically (split, image-space) and stay with preconditioned CG on the normal equations. The image-space Toeplitz preconditioner above is SPD, so it permits plain PCG/PCGLS.
- **Missing cone.** The 0.12 plateau sits in the null space of A. Preconditioners change the convergence rate, not the limit. Gains for laminography are therefore in iterations to plateau, not in the plateau itself, unless priors (nonnegativity/TV, Q2) are added.
- **Inner alignment solve (nonnegative Huber-TV FISTA).** A diagonal or Fourier preconditioner in FISTA changes the prox metric. The nonnegativity prox stays trivial under a *diagonal* (SIRT-type row/column-sum) metric, but not under a Fourier metric. Huber-TV is smooth, so it can sit in the gradient, and only the nonnegativity projection needs the P-metric. Options: diagonal SIRT scaling (safe, cheap), or Nyström/Toeplitz preconditioning with an approximate projection (inexact). This is an inference; no cited paper tests it with nonnegative Huber-TV in 3D.

### Gaps
- I found no arXiv paper that benchmarks ramp/Fourier preconditioning or BA-GMRES specifically on **laminography** or tilted parallel-beam geometry. The laminography transfer above is an inference.
- I found no 2022–2026 arXiv paper on "preconditioned Landweber with ramp filter" proper beyond 2103.10329 (older; SPIE paper posted 2021). Diagonal SIRT preconditioning theory sits in the Hansen/Jørgensen SIAM book (2021) and AIR Tools II (Numer. Algorithms, not arXiv). I did not obtain quantitative SIRT-vs-CGLS numbers for limited-angle data from arXiv.
- Nyström and polynomial preconditioners were tested only in 2D CT or MRI. Their 3D memory cost (K extra volumes) at 256³ is untested.

## Q2. Krylov methods for unmatched projector pairs (AB/BA-GMRES, GKB) and hybrid/flexible regularised Krylov methods (nonnegativity, TV)

### Takeaway
2025–2026 work (Sabaté Landman, Pasha, Gazzola, Chung, Alsubhi) extends the AB/BA framework with automatic Tikhonov parameter choice (hybrid ABBA-GMRES, AB/BA-GKB), so semi-convergence is no longer stopping-sensitive. Flexible and inexact Golub–Kahan methods handle ℓp, TV-like and weighted fidelity terms. In 3D CBCT (TIGRE), Krylov methods reached SIRT/OS-SART quality in about 30 iterations, roughly 6–11× faster in wall-clock.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2602.17892**, "Hybrid ABBA-GMRES for Unmatched Backprojectors in Large Scale X-Ray Computerized Tomography", Ryan Bentley, Mirjeta Pasha, Malena Sabaté Landman, Luisa Yang, Jeffery Zhang (2026). Method: Tikhonov regularisation is added inside the projected AB-/BA-GMRES problem, with λ chosen per iteration by the L-curve or GCV. Hybrid LSMR and hybrid BA-GMRES are equivalent in the matched case. Tests: 2D only, with ASTRA GPU projectors (RTX A2000 laptop GPU) and Re::Solve C++ GPU implementations. Cases: a 180-angle × 128-pixel sparse-matrix example; a 128² Shepp–Logan, parallel, 50 views, 2.5% noise; a 1024² Logan phantom, fan-flat, 50 views, 1.5% noise (A ∈ ℝ^{51200×1048576}). Result: hybrid AB-GMRES with the L-curve "completely stabilizes at the minimum error". Hybrid methods mitigate semi-convergence and give higher quality and more stable stopping than non-hybrid ones. Restarted variants are also studied. — [arXiv:2602.17892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17892)
- **arXiv:2606.31153**, "GKB Methods for X-Ray Computed Tomography with an Unmatched Back Projector", Abdulmajeed Alsubhi (2026). Method: AB-GKB and BA-GKB, preconditioned Golub–Kahan bidiagonalisation for unmatched pairs, with a FLOP-cost comparison against existing methods. Claim: "greater resilience" against semi-convergence than other Krylov methods, validated on several CT problems. I did not extract quantitative numbers. — [arXiv:2606.31153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31153)
- **arXiv:2211.14212**, "On Krylov Methods for Large Scale CBCT Reconstruction", Malena Sabaté Landman, Ander Biguri, Sepideh Hatamikia, Richard Boardman, John Aston, Carola-Bibiane Schönlieb (2022; Phys. Med. Biol. 2023). Method: CGLS, LSQR, LSMR, hybrid LSQR, and TV via IRN (CGLS inner iterations) and hybrid fLSQR, all implemented in TIGRE for 3D CBCT. Results: on synthetic data, 10–20 Krylov iterations give a good reconstruction, with semi-convergence by 60. On real data at comparable quality, Krylov methods at 30 iterations finish within **35 s**, against SIRT 150 iterations in **3 min 50 s** and OS-SART in **6 min 30 s** (~6.6–11×). On a second dataset: Krylov 15 s, SIRT-150 1 min 30 s, OS-SART 1 min 50 s. The TV-regularised IRN used 15 inner iterations per cycle; about 2 outer cycles (30 total iterations) were enough for one example. Hardware: GTX 1070. — [arXiv:2211.14212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14212)
- **arXiv:2510.18865**, "New flexible and inexact Golub-Kahan algorithms for inverse problems", Malena Sabaté Landman, Silvia Gazzola (2025). Flexible and inexact GK factorisations with iteration-dependent preconditioners on the residual space handle general (reweighted, p-norm) data fidelities. Tested on deblurring and CT and reported competitive with popular methods. — [arXiv:2510.18865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18865)
- **arXiv:2510.18853**, "Flexible inner-product free Krylov methods for inverse problems", Malena Sabaté Landman (2025). A flexible generalised Hessenberg method with iteration-dependent preconditioning. It is inner-product free, which enables low-precision arithmetic, and has randomised sketch-and-solve variants for ℓp (0 < p ≤ 1) regularisation. — [arXiv:2510.18853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18853)
- **arXiv:2510.11237**, "Randomized flexible Krylov methods for ℓp regularization", Malena Sabaté Landman, Yuji Nakatsukasa (2025). Flexible Krylov–Tikhonov for IRLS ℓ2–ℓ1, using sketch-and-solve to cut the per-iteration cost growth, plus "sketch-to-precondition" for inner–outer schemes when memory-bound. — [arXiv:2510.11237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11237)
- **arXiv:2306.08499**, "Flexible Krylov Methods for Group Sparsity Regularization", Julianne Chung, Malena Sabaté Landman (2023). Hybrid and iteratively reweighted flexible Krylov methods for group sparsity. — [arXiv:2306.08499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08499)
- **arXiv:2508.20269**, "Randomized Krylov methods for inverse problems", Julianne Chung, Silvia Gazzola (2025). A randomised Golub–Kahan method (sketched inner products, via randomised Gram–Schmidt) for rectangular A, with hybrid variants that choose λ automatically. Tested on deblurring and seismic tomography. — [arXiv:2508.20269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20269)
- **arXiv:2309.15759**, "Recycling MMGKS for large-scale dynamic and streaming data", Mirjeta Pasha, Eric de Sturler, Misha E. Kilmer (2023). Majorisation–minimisation generalised Krylov subspace (MM-GKS) for ℓq-of-gradient (TV-like) regularisation. Recycling compresses and retains important subspace directions, keeping memory bounded for large problems that need many expansion steps. — [arXiv:2309.15759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15759)
- **arXiv:2306.15049**, "Subspace Recycling for Sequences of Shifted Systems with Applications in Image Recovery", Misha E. Kilmer, Eric de Sturler (2023). Krylov subspace recycling across sequences of related (shifted) systems. — [arXiv:2306.15049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15049)
- **arXiv:2501.12497**, "Efficient Dynamic Image Reconstruction with motion estimation", Toluwani Okunola, Mirjeta Pasha, Misha Kilmer, Melina Freitag (2025). Joint dynamic reconstruction with motion estimation in a Krylov/recycling framework (abstract-level; numbers not extracted). — [arXiv:2501.12497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12497)
- **arXiv:2110.13526** **[older, 2021]**, "Software Implementation of the Krylov Methods Based Reconstruction for the 3D Cone Beam CT Operator", Vojtěch Kulvait, Georg Rose. Open-source 3D cone-beam CGLS/LSQR. — [arXiv:2110.13526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13526)

### Inferences
- **Laminography.** Hybrid LSQR/LSMR (matched Joseph pair), or hybrid BA-GMRES if TomoJAX's Pallas backprojector is not the exact adjoint, would remove the "how many CGLS iterations" choice. The 256³ gate then becomes a question of reaching the plateau quickly, not of stopping at the right time. Combined with a filtered B (Q1), this is the most promising non-multilevel replacement for plain CGLS.
- **Adjointness.** If TomoJAX's Pallas backprojector differs from autodiff-exact Joseph Aᵀ (for example a voxel-driven Pallas adjoint kernel), CGLS is formally ill-defined, and Sidky et al. saw divergence after ~20 iterations with an unmatched pair. Check the adjoint mismatch ‖⟨Ax,y⟩ − ⟨x,By⟩‖. If it is non-trivial, BA-GMRES (image-space Krylov basis, k·N³ floats of storage, e.g. 20 vectors × 256³ × 4 B ≈ 1.3 GB) or AB/BA-GKB are the principled choices.
- **TV and nonnegativity inside the inner alignment solve.** IRN/flexible-Krylov TV (2211.14212, 2510.18865) converged in about 30 total projector applications for CBCT. This is comparable to TomoJAX's 20 FISTA iterations per outer step, and could give a lower objective per projector call. Flexible Krylov does not natively enforce nonnegativity. Gazzola has nonnegative flexible-Krylov work (search snippet only, no arXiv ID verified), so a projection or clipping step would be needed.
- **Joint alignment.** Krylov **recycling** (2309.15759, 2306.15049) targets sequences of slowly changing systems. Successive alignment outer steps change A slightly (pose updates), so recycling the image-space subspace across outer steps is the Krylov analogue of FISTA warm-starting. This application is an inference; none of the papers tests pose-varying tomography operators.

### Gaps
- All hybrid ABBA/GKB tests are 2D. There is no 3D or laminography evidence.
- I found no arXiv paper on flexible Golub–Kahan with **nonnegativity plus TV** in 3D CT with timing numbers. The verified flexible-Krylov papers deal with ℓp and TV-like reweighting, not hard constraints.
- Alsubhi 2606.31153: quantitative results not extracted (abstract only).

## Q3. Multigrid, multilevel and coarse-to-fine schemes for tomography, with convergence evidence

### Takeaway
Coarse-to-fine initialisation (cascadic) and true multilevel corrections (MGOPT-like, IML FISTA) both have recent evidence. Kumar & Donatelli report about a 5× runtime reduction from a 3-level coarse-to-fine scheme on real nano-CT. Vanmaele/Elshiaty/Petra show multilevel beating L-BFGS-B far from the solution on ASTRA parallel-beam discrete tomography. IML FISTA gives a convergence-guaranteed multilevel FISTA that maps directly onto TomoJAX's inner FISTA solve. This is consistent with TomoJAX's own finding that coarse-to-fine CGLS rescues the 256³ laminography cell.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2603.28756**, Kumar & Donatelli (2026). Hierarchical multi-resolution: grids Ω₀ … Ω_L. Each level is solved, Lanczos-interpolated to the next finer grid, and used as the initialisation there. "Three hierarchical grid levels are used, starting from a grid of size (N/4 × N/4), doubling the resolution at each stage". Real Tomobank nano-CT, detector 2048×2448, coarse grid 611×611: "the total runtime is reduced by approximately a factor of 5". The ablation ran without FBP initialisation, to isolate the hierarchy effect. Distributed scaling for a 2048×2447×2447 volume on NERSC Perlmutter: about 30 min on 16 nodes, under 9 min on 128 nodes. — [arXiv:2603.28756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28756)
- **arXiv:2505.11104**, "Multilevel Optimization: Geometric Coarse Models and Convergence Analysis", Ferdinand Vanmaele, Yara Elshiaty, Stefania Petra (2025). Method: geometric (not algebraic) multilevel coarse models that give fine-grid descent directions, with convergence analysis under strong convexity and L-smoothness, extended to **box constraints**. Test: discrete tomography with an ASTRA parallel-beam matrix at equidistant angles on [0, π), 6 levels (coarsest grid 32×32), 200 outer iterations at the finest level and 1 at coarse levels, coarse corrections by gradient descent with 2^ℓ iterations. Result: the multilevel approach "reduces the objective more rapidly than L-BFGS" and "converges rapidly when far from the solution", performing competitively overall. Near the solution, superlinear methods (L-BFGS) "prove more effective". Adding more levels improves performance. CPU MATLAB. — [arXiv:2505.11104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11104)
- **arXiv:2304.13329**, "IML FISTA: A Multilevel Framework for Inexact and Inertial Forward-Backward. Application to Image Restoration", Guillaume Lauga, Elisa Riccietti, Nelly Pustelnik, Paulo Gonçalves (2023; SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2024). Multilevel inertial/inexact forward–backward and FISTA, with coarse corrections built from the Moreau envelope of the nonsmooth term. Proves the convergence rate and iterate convergence in the convex case, and "considerably accelerates" one-level FISTA on large images. Tested on restoration, not CT. — [arXiv:2304.13329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13329)
- **arXiv:2210.15940**, "Multilevel fista for image restoration", same authors (2022). The earlier conference version: Moreau-envelope coarse models, outperforming classical FISTA on large images. — [arXiv:2210.15940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15940)
- **arXiv:2403.13385**, "A multilevel framework for accelerating uSARA in radio-interferometric imaging", Lauga, Repetti, Riccietti, Pustelnik, Gonçalves, Wiaux (2024). The same multilevel FB framework applied to a Fourier-sampling inverse problem with irregular sampling, the closest analogue to irregular CT geometries among the multilevel papers. — [arXiv:2403.13385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13385)
- **arXiv:2608.17123**, "Wavelet-based multilevel framework for ℓ1-regularized image deblurring", Danyh Tolah, Malena I. Español, Misha E. Kilmer (2026). Wavelet-space multilevel for ℓ1 problems (abstract-level only). — [arXiv:2608.17123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17123)
- **arXiv:2606.24567**, "Multilevel Stochastic Plug-and-Play for Sparse-View CT Reconstruction", Antoine De Paepe, Alexandre Bousse, Dimitris Visvikis (2026). Multilevel steps are performed in wavelet MRA approximation spaces, so the coarse-level prior-coherence correction "vanish[es] in expectation" and needs no extra denoiser calls. Quality is comparable to state of the art "while substantially reducing runtime" on sparse-view CT. — [arXiv:2606.24567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24567)
- **arXiv:2103.10329** **[older]**, Myers et al.: full-multigrid preconditioned Landweber, giving acceptable images in 1–2 fine iterations (details in Q1). It cites 1990s–2000s nonlinear multigrid tomography (Bouman & Sauer 1992; Köstler/Popa/Prümmer/Rüde algebraic full multigrid for ART, 2005–2006), which is not on arXiv. — [arXiv:2103.10329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10329)

### Inferences
- TomoJAX's coarse-to-fine CGLS is a **cascadic** scheme (coarse solution as fine initial guess). The literature suggests two upgrades:
  1. **Two-grid / MGOPT correction**: return to the coarse level after fine smoothing, using a coarse model with a first-order coherence term (2505.11104, with box constraints so it is compatible with nonnegativity).
  2. Using the **preconditioned** solver as the fine-level smoother (Myers).

  Vanmaele et al. show that multilevel helps most far from the solution. This matches the laminography cell, where 256³ CGLS from zero is far from the plateau.
- **Inner alignment FISTA.** IML FISTA is the most directly transferable: it keeps FISTA, adds coarse corrections via the Moreau envelope of the nonsmooth term (TomoJAX's nonnegativity indicator, whose Moreau envelope is the squared distance to the orthant), and the multiresolution alignment pyramid already supplies the coarse operators. With alignment, the coarse-level geometry must use the same pose parameters (rescaled shifts), so the coarse model stays coherent.
- **Missing cone.** Coarse levels also have a missing cone, so multilevel methods speed convergence to the same plateau. They do not lower the 0.12 floor.

### Gaps
- No verified arXiv paper applies multilevel optimisation to **laminography or limited-angle 3D** with error-vs-time numbers. 2505.11104 is 2D parallel-beam discrete tomography, and 2603.28756 is parallel-beam sparse-angle.
- The Kumar–Donatelli 5× is a single dataset, read off a convergence figure, with no error-at-equal-time table.
- IML FISTA has no CT experiments.

## Q4. Stochastic and subset methods (SPDHG, OS-SART, SVRG/SAGA, ProxSkip, line-search SGD) and their real wall-clock gains

### Takeaway
Subset and variance-reduced methods give large early-iteration speedups. ProxSkip with variance reduction (ProxSVRGSkip) reported 5–20× over non-skipped counterparts, on CPU. FB-LISA on GPU for a 560³ walnut CBCT reached the best relative error within 120 s. Most gains come from fewer full-data passes early on. Evidence on GPU for 3D limited-angle or laminography is thin, and TomoJAX's Huber-TV is smooth, so proximal-skipping benefits are smaller.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2602.09527**, "Split, Skip and Play: Variance-Reduced ProxSkip for Tomography Reconstruction is Extremely Fast", Evangelos Papoutsellis, Zeljko Kereta, Kostas Papafitsoros (2026). Method: random skipping of the expensive prox (TV prox computed with FGP inner iterations) combined with variance-reduced subset gradients (ProxSAGA, ProxSVRG, ProxLSVRG and their "Skip" versions). Problem: min ‖Ax − b‖² + αTV(x) + ι_{x≥0}, plus a PnP-BM3D variant. Data: parallel-beam 2D, a real chemical-imaging slice (400 angles, 347 detector pixels) and a simulated foam phantom (400 views). Subsets N ∈ {10, 50, 100, 200, 400}, skip probability p ∈ {0.01, …, 0.5}. Results: "5×–20×" speed-ups over non-skipped counterparts. About 20× against ISTA (21× with 100 inner TV iterations). ProxSVRGSkip vs ProxSVRG gave 1.65–2.33× wall-clock: 20.8 s against 103.9 s to reach ε = 10⁻⁵ (TV, 10 inner iterations). **Hardware: Apple M2 Pro CPU, no GPU.** — [arXiv:2602.09527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09527)
- **arXiv:2605.12085**, "A Line–Search–Based Stochastic Gradient Method for 3D Computed Tomography", Tatiana A. Bubba, Elena Morotti, Federica Porta, Valeria Ruggiero, Ilaria Trombini (2026). - **arXiv:1706.04957** **[older, 2017, foundational]**, "Stochastic Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient Algorithm with Arbitrary Sampling and Imaging Applications", Antonin Chambolle, Matthias J. ### Inferences
- **GPU wall-clock.** Subset methods help when per-view kernels stay efficient at small batch sizes. On a JAX/Pallas GPU projector, very small subsets (1–10 views of 180) may under-occupy the GPU. Batch sizes of about 10–30 views per step are likely the practical sweet spot (inference; FB-LISA's growing-batch rule is one principled schedule).
- **Inner alignment solve.** Subsets map naturally onto per-view pose parameters: each view's data residual already exists for alignment gradients. An SVRG/SAGA-type volume update over view subsets, warm-started across outer steps, is plausible. Because Huber-TV is smooth and the only nonsmooth term is the nonnegativity projection (cheap), ProxSkip's main gain (skipping an expensive TV prox) mostly does not apply. The relevant part of 2602.09527 is the variance-reduced subset gradient.
- **Limited angle and laminography.** Uniform view sampling is important. Ordered or contiguous subsets in limited angle would bias each step towards a narrow angular band (inference consistent with FB-LISA's uniform random sampling).

### Gaps
- I found no arXiv study giving SPDHG or SVRG GPU wall-clock numbers for **3D laminography or limited-angle** CT.
- The ProxSkip numbers are CPU-only 2D. FB-LISA is a single dataset in MATLAB on a small GPU.

## Q5. Analytic/FBP-type and learned initialisation, laminography FBP filters, and warm starts in joint alignment

### Takeaway
FBP (ramp-filtered backprojection) initialisation reliably halves the initial residual and saves tens of iterations (20–25 in Kumar & Donatelli). Laminography-specific analytic FBP exists (Fourier-based, O(N³ log N)). A 2026 learned-filter FBP for 45° cone-beam laminography reaches iterative-like quality on real data and is a cheap warm start. Learned initial guesses ("Deep Guess") cut MBIR iterations in sparse-view CT. For joint alignment, prior work interleaves single reconstruction iterations with alignment updates, with large gains.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2603.28756**, Kumar & Donatelli (2026). Initialisation by ramp-filtered backprojection (Fourier-domain ramp filter on each projection, then R*): "The initial residual is approximately a factor of two lower compared to constant initialization, effectively reducing the optimization effort by 20–25 iterations." FBP initialisation "maintains a consistent advantage throughout the optimization". This holds even though limited-angle FBP has artefacts. — [arXiv:2603.28756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28756)
- **arXiv:2602.13048**, "Data-Driven Filter Design for Flexible and Noise-Robust Tomographic Imaging", Hamid Fathi, Alexander Skorikov, Tristan van Leeuwen (2026). Method: learn FBP filters and projection weights (linear, a regularised pseudo-inverse with existence/uniqueness/stability results) for parallel, fan, elliptical and **3D circular cone-beam laminography**. Laminography uses angle-dependent 2D filters and 2D weights. Laminography synthetic results: 40×400×400 three-layer flat phantoms, 100 projections over 360°, 512² detector, ϕ = 45°, SOD 5, SDD 20. Learned FBP MSE **0.094 ± 0.002**, SSIM **0.246 ± 0.004**; FDK MSE **0.160 ± 0.004**, SSIM **0.059 ± 0.003** (Table 4). Under tilt mismatch of up to ±5°, SSIM "slightly declines" while MSE improves at higher angles. On a real cone-beam laminography LEGO scan, learned FBP "closely match[es] the NAG solution", where NAG is Nesterov-accelerated gradient iterative reconstruction, while FDK misses structures. The authors also note that analytic laminography FBP derivations "are mathematically involved, highly dependent on specific acquisition setups, and often lead to strong artifacts due to the missing-cone". — [arXiv:2602.13048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13048)
- **arXiv:2401.11101**, "Laminography as a tool for imaging large-size samples with high resolution", Viktor Nikitin, Gregg Wildenberg, Alberto Mittone, Pavel Shevchenko, Alex Deriy, Francesco De Carlo (2024). APS 2-BM laminography pipeline with "a fast reconstruction algorithm with low computational complexity" on multi-GPU, used for about 12 TB of mouse-brain data. Per the search summary, a Fourier-based laminography reconstruction reduces complexity to O(N³ log N). — [arXiv:2401.11101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11101)
- **arXiv:2511.01893**, "mLR: Scalable Laminography Reconstruction based on Memoization", Bin Ma, Viktor Nikitin, Xi Wang, Tekin Bicer, Dong Li (2025). ADMM-FFT, the iterative laminography solver with USFFT-based forward/adjoint and TV, is accelerated by memoising repeated FFTs across iterations, with 52.8% average and up to 65.4% performance improvement, scaled to 2K³. These are systems-level gains, not iteration-count gains. — [arXiv:2511.01893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01893)
- **arXiv:2412.01703**, "Deep Guess acceleration for explainable image reconstruction in sparse-view CT", Elena Loli Piccolomini, Davide Evangelista, Elena Morotti (2024). A trained network provides the starting guess for a proximal MBIR solver on a non-convex model, computing "an interpretable solution image in a few iterations". It beats the purely variational counterpart in (very) sparse-view settings, with a ground-truth-free variant and noise-robustness tests. — [arXiv:2412.01703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01703)
- **arXiv:2604.01844**, "FaCT-GS: Fast and Scalable CT Reconstruction with Gaussian Splatting", Pawel Tomasz Pieta, Rasmus Juul Pedersen, Sina Borgi, Jakob Sauer Jørgensen, Jens Wenzel Andreasen, Vedrana Andersen Dahl (2026). Rapid fitting of Gaussians to existing volumes serves as a warm-start prior. More than 4× faster than prior GS-CT on 512² projections and more than 13× faster on 2k projections. It notes that warm-starting SIRT/FISTA from a volumetric prior "can substantially accelerate convergence depending on the degree of similarity". — [arXiv:2604.01844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01844)
- **arXiv:2511.02481**, "NOWS: Neural Operator Warm Starts for Accelerating Iterative Solvers", Eshaghi et al. (2025). Learned initial guesses for CG/GMRES give up to 90% computational-time reduction while keeping solver guarantees. This is in PDE contexts, not CT. — [arXiv:2511.02481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02481)
- **arXiv:2402.12141**, "Fast deep learning based reconstruction for limited angle tomography", Knut Salomonsson, Eric Oldgren, Emanuel Ström, Ozan Öktem (2024). FNO back-projection with moment-condition sinogram extrapolation for limited angle, at FBP-comparable runtime (Helsinki Tomography Challenge 2022). — [arXiv:2402.12141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12141)
- **arXiv:2510.04369**, "The method of the approximate inverse for limited-angle CT", Bernadette Hahn, Gael Rigaud, Richard Schmähl (2025). Precomputed limited-angle reconstruction kernels (LARK) avoid FBP streaks but inherit severe ill-conditioning. The CLARK variant adds spectral filtering and edge-preserving denoising. Validated on synthetic and real (2D) data. This is a model-driven analytic alternative to FBP for limited-angle initialisation. — [arXiv:2510.04369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04369)
- **arXiv:2503.19248**, "Limited-angle x-ray nano-tomography with machine-learning enabled iterative reconstruction engine", Chonghang Zhao, Mingyuan Ge, Xiaogang Yang, Yong S. Chu, Hanfei Yan (2025). ADMM with a CNN regulariser handles missing wedges over 100°, where conventional methods fail. — [arXiv:2503.19248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19248)
- **Joint alignment warm starts (non-arXiv primary).** Gürsoy et al., "Rapid alignment of nanotomography data using joint iterative reconstruction and reprojection" (Sci. Rep. 2017), interleave one reprojection-alignment step after each reconstruction iteration instead of running full reconstructions per alignment step. Per the search summary, this gives a residual alignment error "a factor of a thousand smaller" and reaches the same reconstruction error "in less than half the number of iterations". — [Nature Sci. Rep.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-12141-9). Odstrčil-type follow-up: [Joint Iterative Fast Projection Matching, Sci. Rep. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-62949-1).
- **arXiv:1612.05515** **[older, 2016]**, "On the crucial impact of the coupling projector-backprojector in iterative tomographic reconstruction", Filippo Arcadu, Marco Stampanoni, Federica Marone. This is directly relevant to the matched/unmatched question for iterative solvers. — [arXiv:1612.05515](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05515)

### Inferences
- **Laminography matrix cells.** Starting CGLS/BA-GMRES from a laminography FBP (2D-ramp-filtered backprojection along the tilted detector, or a learned filter) should save on the order of tens of iterations at 256³, by analogy with the 20–25 iterations of Kumar–Donatelli. Combining FBP initialisation, coarse-to-fine and a filtered/Toeplitz preconditioner is complementary. Kumar–Donatelli report their gains separately and do not quantify the combination.
- **Inner alignment FISTA.** Warm-starting is already in place. The cited joint alignment literature supports *fewer* inner iterations per outer step: 1 iteration per alignment update in Gürsoy et al., against TomoJAX's 20. That reallocates the projector budget towards more outer pose updates. The risk is pose updates computed against an under-converged volume at fine levels. A schedule of more inner iterations at coarse levels and fewer at fine levels, with an FBP/filtered-backprojection volume as the very first initialisation, is the evidence-consistent design (inference).
- **FBP initialisation under misalignment.** An FBP initialisation of misaligned data embeds streaks and doubling. It is still a better start than zero for low frequencies (Kumar–Donatelli's residual argument), but its high-frequency content should be damped, for example with a Hann-windowed ramp, before alignment begins (inference).

### Gaps
- I found no arXiv paper that quantifies **warm-start effects inside joint alignment** for laminography, or the trade-off between inner iterations per outer step and final pose accuracy, beyond Gürsoy 2017 (Sci. Rep., not arXiv, numbers via search snippet).
- Analytic laminography FBP filter derivations on arXiv were not located with verifiable IDs. Classic laminography FBP (e.g. Voropaev et al. 2016; Helfen et al.) predates the window and was not verified on arXiv here.

## Q6. Fast, accurate handling of anisotropic voxels and shifted detectors in FBP-like methods (and the failing anisotropic shifted 64³ cell)

### Takeaway
I found almost no 2022–2026 arXiv work dedicated to anisotropic voxels or shifted detectors in FBP. The relevant levers are filter optimisation (closed-form MSE-optimal or learned filters) and replacing FBP with a few iterations of an FBP-preconditioned Krylov method. That approach beat 180-view FBP RMSE by about 40% in Sidky et al. Because ASTRA also fails the same cell, the failure is probably discretisation-limited rather than solver-limited.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2408.06471**, "Optimized filter functions for filtered back projection reconstructions", Matthias Beckmann, Judith Nickel (2024). A closed-form filter minimising the expected squared L² error of FBP under noise, adapted to finitely many measurements. It needs no training data and is an "out-of-the-box solution". Supported by simulated and real CT experiments. — [arXiv:2408.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06471)
- **arXiv:2602.13048** (Q5): learned FBP filters and weights adapt "naturally to the noise level and acquisition geometry", including non-standard trajectories. They are a regularised linear pseudo-inverse, and FBP/FDK improvements were consistent across 2D and 3D cases. — [arXiv:2602.13048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13048)
- **arXiv:2201.07408** (Q1): 4 iterations of BA-GMRES with the ramp-filtered backprojector reached image RMSE 0.0210 against 0.0347 for FBP at the same 180 views, about 39% lower, at roughly 4–5× FBP cost. — [arXiv:2201.07408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07408)
- **arXiv:2010.14205** **[older, 2020]**, "Reconstruction of Voxels with Position- and Angle-Dependent Weightings", Lina Felsner, Tobias Würfl, Christopher Syben, Philipp Roser, Alexander Preuhs, Andreas Maier, Christian Riess. This addresses position- and angle-dependent weighting in voxel reconstruction, the general setting for shifted or offset geometries. — [arXiv:2010.14205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14205)
- Offset-detector FDK weighting: the search found descriptions of offset-detector weights in FBP, and of the same weights applied to the data-fidelity residual in iterative reconstruction, in a CBCT scatter-rejection pipeline paper (arXiv:2308.09095, "2D antiscatter grid and scatter sampling based CBCT pipeline for image guided radiation therapy", Bayat, Ruan, Miften, Altunbas, 2023). This is application-specific clinical CBCT, not a method paper. — [arXiv:2308.09095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09095)

### Inferences
- **The failing 64³ anisotropic shifted cell** (FBP 0.178 vs a 0.15 gate; CGLS, SIRT and ASTRA also fail). Every method failing, including ASTRA, points to a **model and discretisation floor**: sharp structured edges at 64³ with anisotropic voxels, where Joseph/voxel interpolation and detector sampling alias, plus a shifted detector giving incomplete or redundant coverage at the field-of-view edge. Iteration count is unlikely to be the problem. Solver-side levers that could plausibly move it, all untested inferences:
  1. A **few iterations of FBP-filtered BA-GMRES or LSQR initialised from FBP**, since iterative refinement beat FBP by about 40% RMSE in Sidky et al.
  2. An **MSE-optimal or regularised filter** (Beckmann–Nickel) in place of Ram-Lak/Shepp-Logan, which trades high-frequency noise against bias.
  3. **Nonnegativity plus TV** for sharp structured phantoms, because structure-promoting priors are what beat the linear floor in the sharp-edge regime.
  4. **Supersampled or anisotropy-aware projectors** (more rays per detector pixel, or footprint/distance-driven models for anisotropic voxels) to reduce the model-mismatch floor. This is a forward-model fix, not a solver fix.
- **Shifted detectors.** Redundancy weighting (Parker/offset weights) applied to the residual inside iterative methods is the standard way to keep FBP-equivalent weighting consistent with LSQR-type solvers (inference from the offset-weight description above).

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 arXiv paper was found that specifically analyses **anisotropic voxel** effects on FBP accuracy or iterative convergence. This is a genuine literature gap in arXiv search results, and the relevant work is probably in IEEE TMI or Med. Phys., not on arXiv.
- All arXiv IDs in this document were API-verified (titles and authors match). Content claims for 2010.14205, 2308.09095, 1612.05515 and 2410.09837 rest on abstracts or search excerpts, not full-text reading.
