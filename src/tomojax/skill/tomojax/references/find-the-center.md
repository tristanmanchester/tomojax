# Find the rotation center

1. Inspect the geometry, detector pixel size, and angle coverage. Correct raw frames before fitting, apply `Rings` or `RejectViews` if strong rings or broken frames are present, and confirm the selected row band has transmitted sample signal across the turn (dark or empty bands have no axis to measure).
2. Call `align` with `fit="center"` and no joint options. On an unposed, unrolled, well-sampled axial parallel scan, this measures the rotation axis directly across the full detector width from the half-turn sinogram (`focus_px`, `binning`, `views`, `pairs`) or from the strip shared by opposing views of a full turn (`estimator`, `pairs`, `rows`, `overlap_px`) at fractional-pixel resolution, returning the corrected scan with no working image (`method="sinogram-focus"`). On a full-turn acquisition with a detector-derived grid (including a saved corrected scan), the returned scan's grid expands to the far detector edge; a grid stated by the file or caller keeps its extent. On cone beam, laminography, posed, rolled, or sparsely sampled scans, it runs the joint fit. Pass `method="joint"` (or use `describe` for joint settings) if you want a joint image-and-center solve on a parallel scan, or `method="opposite-views"` for a pair-only check.
3. Verify the result by reconstructing a slice or running a `center_sweep` preview around the fitted center (offsets are signed horizontal detector pixels on the input scan). Look for sharp single edges. If features still look doubled, check for unilluminated rows, bad flats, detector roll (`fit=("translations", "roll")`), or per-view motion (`fit="translations"`).
4. Report the fitted center with its units, sign convention, and diagnostics (`info["calibration"]["center"]` and `info["diagnostics"]`). Pass the alignment's `.scan` member to `reconstruct`.
