# Reconstruct a laboratory CT acquisition

1. Inspect the acquisition directory. Open the acquisition metadata file and check its angle range, pixel size, source distances and detector dimensions. On a saved scan that incorporated Zeiss corrections, `apply_poses=false` raises an error; open the original `.txrm` with `apply_poses=false` for nominal geometry, or clear rigid poses with `edit_scan` (`poses=null`).
2. Identify flats, darks and exposure times. Open raw counts as frames. Record any assumed calibration value and use a correction preview to compare raw and corrected rows before applying corrections.
3. Check the rotation center. Fit detector roll when the data supports it. Inspect the aligned scan and retain its geometry for reconstruction.
4. Check the reconstruction plan with `dry_run`, then reconstruct. Inspect central and off-center slices for doubled edges, rings, and streaks, and check `outside_fraction` and `interior_shift` in `truncation`. FDK continues the rows with a fitted cylinder. Iterative cone solvers estimate the projection from outside the grid with a coarse fit to those rows. Read `outside_grid` for its share of the measured line integral. Pass `config: {"exterior": "zero"}` with an explicit method to assume zero outside. Save the volume and manifest. Use `export` (with `dry_run=true` first) for ImageJ TIFF, OME-TIFF, TIFF slices, raw binary, MetaImage, or AmiraMesh.
