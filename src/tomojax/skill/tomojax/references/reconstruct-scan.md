# Reconstruct a scan

1. For a file, use `inspect` to check projection shape, angle coverage, detector spacing and geometry, then `open` it. For a live scan, start from its handle. Read `loading` in the report: `full_turn` marks a closed axial parallel orbit (with `axis_to_edge_px`, `field_of_view_px`, `overlap_px`, and `central_gap_px` when the axis is known and roll is a multiple of 90°); `frame_records` means the scan stopped early and loading reads only the frames every record covers; `length_unit: "pixel"` means no pixel size is recorded, so lengths and attenuation are per detector pixel; `center: "not_recorded"` means the center is the detector middle until you fit it; `geometry: "not_recorded"` means the file records no rotation axis, and reconstruction and alignment refuse it until you `open` it with `geometry`, for example `{"kind": "parallel"}` for a synchrotron scan. The `geometry` of every result repeats these as `detector_center_status`, `geometry_status` and `frame_records`.
2. Use `correct` on raw frames to obtain line integrals; include `{"type": "Rings"}` if detector rings appear (it takes no width parameter). Bracketing flat sets default to `flat_weights: "views"`; check `structuredContent.corrections` for the `flat_dark` record, and if a flat-field warning arrives in `notes` (which also notes when a shadow in both flat sets leaves the incident intensity uncertain), inspect the reconstruction and either pass a matching scan's file as `flats` on `open` to borrow clean flats, or pass `flat_weights: "position"` to blend by position alone. Check the center or motion with `align` if needed; for joint fits, check `disturbed_views` and inspect any flagged projections before excluding confirmed ranges with `config: {"exclude_views": [[start, stop], ...]}`. Pass the fitted scan member to reconstruction.
3. Call `reconstruct` with `dry_run=true` to check the method, grid and placement. For a raw acquisition larger than memory, check `reconstruct_slabs` with a destination path (it saves whole-scan `truncation` and iterative stopping diagnostics in `volume.reconstruction_info` and returns them in its summary).
4. Run the reconstruction and inspect its three planes. Check `truncation` (`outside_fraction` and `interior_shift`, plus `outside_grid`, `data_model`, and `background_treatment` for iterative methods; the summary text prints `N% of attenuation beyond the detector` from 1% up, and the iterative background treatment when applied). When material extends past the detector, all seven methods model the exterior by default (`exterior="cylinder"`): `fourier`, `fbp`, and cone-beam FDK continue each detector row with a cylinder model, while `cgls`, `sirt`, `fista`, and `spdhg` subtract a coarse exterior estimate before solving and `mlem` adds its nonnegative estimate to predicted counts. Pass `config: {"exterior": "zero"}` with an explicit `method` to fit the original data directly. An iterative result reports how it ended: `termination` (`projection_discrepancy` or `noise_score` when it stopped at the estimated noise in the data, `iteration_limit` when it ran out of iterations), `effective_iterations` and `early_stop`; FISTA with automatic TV also reports `resolved_tv_weight` and `noise_score_rms`. A run that reached `iteration_limit` may want more iterations. Use `view` to examine off-center structures. Save the result with `save` (which returns the written `paths`) and report the path, method, grid, stopping status, truncation share when nonzero, and visible artifacts.

Workflow `source` fields also accept acquisition paths. To supply measured arrays, open the scan and select `.projections`, or open with `kind=frames` and select `.counts` for raw counts. Use `inspect` with `preview=true` for a first look or a `correction_preview` with `preview` to examine corrected rows.
