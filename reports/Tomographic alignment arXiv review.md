# Coupling-aware Gauss–Newton can unstick TomoJAX's rotations

The literature supports replacing TomoJAX's fixed-volume per-view Gauss–Newton (GN) pose step with a coupled variable-projection (VarPro) / Schur-complement step, but it also says the details decide whether the change works. The closest prior work, van Leeuwen, Maretzke and Batenburg, solves the same 5-DOF parallel-beam problem. It shows that the reduced (Schur-complement) Hessian is never worse conditioned than the joint Hessian. It also shows that running more alignment steps against a frozen volume does not improve accuracy ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). The Linearize-and-Project (LAP) method of Herring, Nagy and Ruthotto solves the same linearised joint system TomoJAX proposes, but eliminates the small motion block rather than the volume. In an MRI motion-correction test it reached motion errors about two orders of magnitude below both block-coordinate descent and VarPro ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)). Hong, Zach and Fitzgibbon show that the defining difference between VarPro and "joint" Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) is that VarPro puts no damping on the eliminated block. In their bundle-adjustment test that difference moves the chance of reaching the best optimum from 4–24% to 94% ([CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hong_Revisiting_the_Variable_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)).

Together these results change five implementation details:

1. Leave the volume increment undamped. TomoJAX's in-progress coupled step requires a strictly positive `gn_volume_damping`.
2. Eliminate the 305-parameter pose block exactly, then iterate only on the volume.
3. Tighten the inner-solve tolerance across outer iterations.
4. Project rigid-object gauge modes out of every pose step. `gauge.py` currently fixes only the mean translation.
5. Switch from alternation to the coupled step only near the solution, with alternation as a fallback ([arXiv:2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)).

The same evidence warns that some of the 0.02° parallel-beam plateau may come from weak identifiability of low-frequency tomographic-angle errors, or from a pose Jacobian that is not accurate enough. A better solver cannot fix either. An analytic, discretisation-exact pose Jacobian built on TomoJAX's exact trilinear integrator is therefore the essential companion change ([arXiv:2606.21405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405), [arXiv:2508.13304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13304)). For the ±3°/±10 px capture-range goal and for laminography, published pipelines rely on staged, per-DOF seeding and multiresolution. None reports per-view three-rotation recovery at TomoJAX's 0.01° gate, so TomoJAX is working beyond the published state of the art there. Throughout, text marked *Inference* is my reasoning from the cited results, not a finding the papers report.

## Fixed-volume Gauss–Newton under-steps exactly where the volume absorbs rotation

TomoJAX's outer loop runs 20 warm-started FISTA iterations, then one per-view GN pose step on a frozen volume. This is block-coordinate descent with inexact blocks: van Leeuwen et al.'s Algorithm 3 with one alignment step per outer iteration. Their Theorem 2.1 shows that the reduced gradient equals the partial pose gradient evaluated at the optimal volume. They identify the reduced Hessian as "the Schur complement of (WᵀW + ∇²h) in the full Hessian". Their Theorem 2.2 proves, by eigenvalue interlacing, that the reduced Hessian's condition number is no worse than the joint Hessian's ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). The fixed-volume step uses J_θᵀJ_θ as its curvature. The Schur complement subtracts J_θᵀA(AᵀA+R)⁻¹AᵀJ_θ from it, and that term is largest in the pose directions a perturbed volume can mimic. *Inference:* the fixed-volume step therefore overestimates curvature and under-steps exactly in those directions. This matches TomoJAX's symptoms. Translations are weakly coupled to volume changes and converge to about 0.001 px. Rotations are strongly coupled and crawl.

The rate data fit this picture. TomoJAX's internal review records anisotropic rotation error shrinking by about 0.80× every 8 outers and laminography by about 0.87× every 8 outers. That is a per-outer contraction of about 0.973 and 0.983, with each GN step cutting the loss by only about 3%. *Inference:* for a locally linear-quadratic two-block problem, exact alternation is block Gauss–Seidel. Its asymptotic rate is the squared cosine of the smallest principal angle between the regularised volume range and the pose-Jacobian range. Contractions of 0.973–0.983 imply principal angles of only about 7.5–9.5°, so some rotation directions are almost reproducible by a volume perturbation. A Schur step is built to remove exactly this. With only 20 inexact FISTA iterations per outer, the true rate is worse than exact block Gauss–Seidel.

Van Leeuwen et al. also rule out the cheapest alternative. Raising the number of fixed-volume alignment iterations per outer to as many as 10 gave "slightly more optimal results but not … a higher accuracy", and slightly larger alignment error ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). More GN pose iterations against a frozen volume do not address the curvature error. The only convergence rate they prove is linear, for reduced-gradient VarPro with linearly tightening inner tolerances. They state that rates for alternating minimisation "have, to the best of our knowledge, not been established" ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)).

LAP adds the strongest head-to-head numbers. It solves min ½‖J_x δx + J_w δw + r₀‖² + α/2‖L(x₀+δx)‖² and eliminates the motion block. It then runs a Krylov solver on the projected image problem. With 5% noise in MRI motion correction, LAP reached motion error **1.59e-4 in 318 matvecs**. Block-coordinate descent (BCD) stalled at 2.27e-2 and VarPro with a gradient-based inner solve at 4.33e-2 after **21,900 matvecs** ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)). In a 2D super-resolution test the three methods reached similar accuracy, but LAP used 66 matvecs against 92 for BCD and 468 for VarPro. The authors conclude that BCD "ignores the coupling between the variable blocks", while LAP "takes it into consideration" ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)).

## Evidence rewrites five details of the variable-projection step

The recommendation in `docs/research/opus-review-2026-10-04.md` is to solve min‖Aδv + J_θδθ − r‖² with damping by CGLS/LSQR on [A, J_θ], keep only δθ, then refresh with FISTA. The working tree already holds a related coupled step, `src/tomojax/alignment/_pose/_coupled_objective.py`, which was untracked when this review was written. It solves the stacked normal equations by Jacobi-preconditioned CG (`solve_coupled_normal`) and damps both blocks (`gn_volume_damping`, default 1e-3, validated as strictly positive; `gn_damping` on poses). It masks KKT-active zero voxels and line-searches on both blocks. The papers below were checked against both designs.

### Damp the pose block, never the volume increment

Hong et al.'s table is the most decision-relevant number in this review. Damping the eliminated (structure) block collapses success from 94% to 24%, even when the structure is re-solved afterwards. Without the re-solve, success is 0–4% ([CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hong_Revisiting_the_Variable_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)). LAP's image block likewise carries only the true regulariser α‖L(x₀+δx)‖², with no LM shift ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)). *Inference:* TomoJAX's coupled step should drop `gn_volume_damping` to zero, or to a numerical floor far below the scale of AᵀA. It should rely on the Huber-TV Hessian (which the working-tree code already adds via `reg_hessian`) to make the volume block positive definite. LM damping belongs only on δθ, ideally adapted by a trust-region gain ratio rather than a fixed 1e-3. Whether 1e-3 is significant depends on how AᵀA is scaled, but the principle in the evidence is categorical: the eliminated block is solved, not damped.

### Eliminate the 305 pose parameters exactly; iterate only on the volume

The two elimination orders give the same δθ when solved exactly, but they differ in cost. With 61 views and 5 DOF there are 305 pose parameters. Because each view's pose touches only its own detector rows, J_θᵀJ_θ is block diagonal with 61 blocks of 5×5. It becomes banded only if the pose smoothness prior couples neighbouring views. *Inference:* following LAP, form this pose block exactly, including LM damping and any smoothness prior. A dense 305×305 Cholesky is trivial. Then run CGLS/LSQR on the projected volume operator (I − J_θ(J_θᵀJ_θ+λI)⁻¹J_θᵀ)A, and back-substitute δθ. Per Krylov iteration this costs one A, one Aᵀ and 61 tiny per-view projections. LAP notes that the projection is a rank-≤p perturbation, so "a good problem-specific preconditioner for J_x should be a suitable preconditioner for the projected operator" ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)).

The working-tree solver instead runs CG on the stacked normal equations with a per-DOF diagonal pose preconditioner. That preconditioner ignores the strong within-view coupling between, for example, in-plane rotation and detector shifts. LAP reports that directly iterating on the joint system "has been observed to converge slowly" ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)). The opposite elimination, forming the dense 305×305 Schur complement S, needs 305 inner volume solves. It is useful as a small-problem oracle and diagnostic (see the next section), not as the production path. If the explicit Schur form is ever wanted at scale, PoVar's power-series inverse ([arXiv:2405.05079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05079)) and Papalia et al.'s matrix-free Schur operator ([arXiv:2512.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07969)) are the tested routes.

### Tighten inner accuracy over outers, and keep regularisation in the eliminated block

Van Leeuwen et al. show that a reconstruction tolerance of ε = 10⁻¹ makes the rotation fit "break down", stalling or diverging, while ε ≤ 10⁻² recovers everything except the tomographic angle. Their linear-rate theorem needs ε_k to decay linearly, for example ε_k = r^k ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). Español and Jeronimo give an explicit per-outer LSQR stopping rule that preserves convergence when the Jacobians are inexact ([arXiv:2402.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08568)). Comerso Salzer et al. extend it to an inexact-LSQR variant with local convergence guarantees ([arXiv:2601.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05224)). LAP warns that low-accuracy inner solves "may considerably degrade the performance of VarPro" ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)).

*Inference:* the fixed `gn_joint_rtol = 1e-4` and the fixed 20 FISTA iterations per outer should both become schedules: loose early, geometrically tighter late. Stopping should key on the change in δθ between Krylov iterations, since δx accuracy matters only through δθ. This is compatible with Gürsoy et al.'s finding that one reconstruction iteration per alignment step suffices early on for translations ([Sci. Rep. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-12141-9)).

Regularisation must stay in the eliminated block. Van Leeuwen et al. found that "choosing α too low may cause the alignment to stall prematurely". They recommend continuation from a large α to a small one ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). *Inference:* with free voxels, 61 views and (in laminography) a missing cone, A has a large near-null space. Without regularisation, the projected pose Jacobian along rotation directions can collapse to near zero, because the volume absorbs the rotation, and the step becomes ill-posed.

### Handle positivity by projected GN, with FISTA as the re-solve

The reduced-gradient formula assumes ∇ₓf = 0 at the inner solution. Active nonnegativity bounds break that assumption, and LAP shows the neglected term can "considerably degrade" VarPro ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)). LAP's remedy is projected GN on both blocks. Non-smooth VarPro is the formal alternative ([arXiv:1601.05011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.05011)). The working-tree code already masks KKT-active zero voxels (`free = (x > 0) | (gx < 0)`). That matches LAP and should stay. Hong's "embedded point iterations" correspond to TomoJAX's warm-started FISTA refresh after the pose update, so the existing nonnegative Huber-TV FISTA is the right re-solve. *Inference:* for the Huber-TV term inside the linear system, the majorise–minimise reweighting of Español and Pasha ([arXiv:2105.14155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14155)) is the principled way to make it quadratic for one GN step. It replaces the exact Hessian of a piecewise function.

### Project gauge modes out of every step

Van Leeuwen et al. list the parallel-beam gauge modes that are "constant transforms of the entire object" ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)):

- the mean tomographic angle;
- a joint sinusoid in the in-plane and nod angles (a uniform axis tilt);
- a sinusoid in the lateral shift (object translation perpendicular to the axis);
- the mean axial shift.

Gauge symmetries make the Schur complement rank-deficient and need explicit handling ([arXiv:2512.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07969)). Weber et al. show that a low objective can coexist with wrong geometry when ambiguity modes remain ([arXiv:2608.18028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18028)).

TomoJAX's verification already fits one common rigid object frame before scoring. The solver, however, fixes only mean translation (`gauge.py`, mode `mean_translation`). The internal review records gauge-fitted parallel rotation stuck at 0.020–0.022° while raw rotation "drifts slowly" and the loss keeps falling. *Inference:* that is the signature of steps leaking into near-null gauge directions. The coupled step should project the full rigid-object gauge subspace out of δθ: 6 DOF in general, minus whatever the 5-DOF detector-shift parameterisation cannot express. This keeps step-length control and trust-region ratios from being spent on unobservable motion. For laminography no paper derives the gauge set. The tilted-axis analogue should therefore be taken from the numerical null space of S on a small problem, not assumed. Correct gauge handling defines the metric properly and leaves the gate unchanged.

### Switch from alternation once the start is good, and keep alternation as a fallback

Okunola et al. give the clearest switching evidence. VarPro "actually converges slightly faster" than alternation from a good start, but oscillates without settling from a poor one. Their recommendation is to "use VarPro when you have a decent initial guess … AltMin when initialization quality is uncertain" ([arXiv:2605.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06336)). Vanmaele et al. reach a parallel conclusion for multilevel against L-BFGS in parallel-beam tomography: cheap first-order or multilevel steps win far from the solution, while superlinear methods win near it ([arXiv:2505.11104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11104)). Hong's 94% basin cuts the other way for bundle adjustment. *Inference:* the conflict dissolves if the pose step is trust-region controlled. Run alternation through the coarse multiresolution levels and the translation-capture phase. Engage the coupled step once translations are converged, which TomoJAX's pilots already achieve. Gate each coupled step by an LM gain ratio, and fall back to an alternation step when the ratio is poor.

Anderson acceleration of the existing outer map is a cheap side experiment. It is competitive with quasi-Newton in seismic inversion ([arXiv:2008.11778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11778)), but I found no tomography-alignment evidence for it, and it cannot recover weakly observable modes.

## Part of the plateau is identifiability and Jacobian precision, not solver speed

The papers that support VarPro also predict where it will not help. Even after the gauge modes are removed, van Leeuwen et al. found that the tomographic angle "remains corrupted by low-frequency errors, which may point to an inherent non-uniqueness". Their alignment error "always exhibits a slight increase after an initial phase of convergence" ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). TomoJAX's parallel cell has the same shape: a 0.02° plateau from outer 24 to 64 while the loss still falls. The anisotropic and laminography cells, by contrast, are still descending linearly. *Inference:* the coupled step should collapse the anisotropic and laminography descent. A residual parallel plateau would then point to observability or Jacobian accuracy, which the internal review already flags as the main uncertainty.

Jacobian accuracy is the more fixable of the two. Haouchat et al. show that with a degree-0 voxel basis "the loss landscape is piecewise constant, thus producing zero gradients that trap the optimizer". A quadratic B-spline basis reduced final geometry error by **55% (0.021 vs 0.048 detector units)** for a 500³, 900-angle parallel-beam case ([arXiv:2606.21405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21405)). TomoJAX's exact trilinear integrator is a degree-1 basis. It is continuous in pose, but its derivative is only piecewise smooth, and TomoJAX currently builds pose columns by central finite differences in FP32. *Inference:* a 0.01° rotation moves a voxel at radius 16–32 px by only about 0.003–0.006 px on the pilot grids (e.g. 32×29×16 anisotropic). A finite-difference stencil with FP32 round-off then sits close to the signal it must resolve. A Schur step built on a noisy J_θ cannot cross that floor.

Jiang et al. derive analytic gradients of forward and back projection with respect to rigid pose, "expressed directly in terms of the forward and back-projection operations themselves". They used 0.77 GB against DiffDRR's 5.74 GB at 256³ and ran about 7.8× faster in 2D/3D registration. They acknowledge an "approximation error due to mismatch between continuous formulation and discrete application" ([arXiv:2508.13304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13304)). *Inference:* TomoJAX can avoid that mismatch. Its per-cell two-point Gauss–Legendre integral is exact for the cubic-in-t trilinear integrand, so it can be differentiated exactly with respect to the ray end points. That yields a pose Jacobian consistent with the forward operator, checked against the existing JAX reference by JVP and finite differences. A degree-2 box-spline mode is the next step if the derivative's piecewise smoothness proves limiting. Haouchat et al.'s 2D exact-basis projector cost ×2.6 (degree 1) and ×4.1 (degree 2) relative to pixels ([arXiv:2503.20907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20907)).

Identifiability calls for diagnosis, not tuning. *Inference:* on a small problem, form S = J_θᵀ(I − A(AᵀA+R)⁻¹Aᵀ)J_θ with one CGLS solve per column. With 305 columns this is cheap at pilot size. The near-null eigenvectors identify the gauge modes and any weakly identifiable modes (for example, smooth tomographic-angle drift). The ratio of J_θᵀJ_θ to S along rotation directions predicts the per-outer alternation rate directly. This is the one diagnostic that separates "coupling" from "observability" without new infrastructure. It also gives laminography its own gauge set, since none is published. Bayesian joint angle/image estimation offers a complementary route to posterior widths on view angles ([arXiv:2104.06919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06919), Uribe, Bardsley, Dong, Hansen, Riis, 2021 **[older]**; Riis, Dong, Hansen, [Inverse Problems 2021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/abf5ba) **[older]**). If weak modes turn out to be physical, the evidence-backed responses are:

- nonnegativity, which van Leeuwen found improves alignment;
- a better-conditioned volume model, such as the B-spline basis above;
- explicit, documented pose priors such as smoothness over view index. Comerso Salzer et al. show that parameter regularisation "prevents degenerate … solutions" ([arXiv:2601.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05224)).

Loosening the 0.01° gate is not one of them.

## Capture range comes from staged pipelines, not one wide basin

The ±10 px translation half of the capture-range target matches published evidence. Gürsoy et al. interleave one reconstruction iteration with phase-correlation realignment. With shifts drawn from U(−10, 10) px, they recovered all axial shifts at sub-pixel accuracy up to 10% noise. Residual alignment error was "a factor of a thousand smaller" than with sequential reconstruct-then-align ([Sci. Rep. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5603591/)). The ±3° rotation half has no published equivalent. Van Leeuwen et al. scaled the initial misalignment by up to 16×. All parameters except the tomographic angle were still recovered, and "if the misalignment exceeds the angular sampling, it becomes exceedingly hard to find the correct projection angles as nearby projections are similar" ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). *Inference:* TomoJAX's pilot uses 61 irregular half-turn views, a mean spacing of about 2.95°. A ±3° in-axis error therefore sits right at the edge where van Leeuwen reports local methods failing. No GN or Schur step will cross that edge without a global stage: a coarse search, a monotonicity or ordering constraint on nominal angles, or a bounded proximal step as in van Leeuwen's Algorithm 2.

Every working pipeline decouples the identifiable sub-problems first. PSI's toolkit chains three stages and covers laminography as well as tomography ([Odstrčil et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.27.036637), with public code on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/3360819)):

1. cross-correlation for an initial guess;
2. a vertical-mass-fluctuation common-line method for the axial shift, independent of horizontal misalignment;
3. multiresolution projection matching for the final refinement.

Its "sub-0.2 pixel RMS" figure comes from a secondary summary only. JI-Faproma estimates in-plane rotation from frequency-domain common lines and the vertical shift from real-space common lines. At 20% noise its errors stay within 0.12°, 0 px and 1 px, in fewer than 15 iterations ([Sci. Rep. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7192921/)). Nikitin et al.'s non-rigid ADMM estimates deformation coarse-to-fine and improved FSC resolution on real data from 192 nm to 127 nm ([arXiv:2008.03375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03375)). Van Leeuwen et al. widen the basin with α-continuation: "a smoother reconstruction … improves the initial convergence of the alignment" ([arXiv:1705.08678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08678)). Thies et al. cut the DOF with spline motion models. They also warn that the model "crucially influences recoverable frequencies", so a low-dimensional pose model must be released to per-view DOF at the finest level ([arXiv:2405.19079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19079)).

*Inference:* the evidence-consistent capture-range design for TomoJAX runs in four stages:

1. Closed-form, data-only seeds for 2 of 5 DOF: the existing phase-correlation translation seed plus common-line axial-shift and in-plane-rotation estimates.
2. Multiresolution alternation with large-α Huber-TV and, optionally, a spline pose model at coarse levels.
3. A global stage for the tomographic angle when errors approach the angular spacing.
4. The coupled Schur step at the finest levels, where Okunola et al. show VarPro is safe.

Cryo-ET contributes one transferable idea: Markerfree "limits the number of images participating in reconstruction while improving their quality" ([Structure 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2026.01.007)). *Inference:* realigning a view against a reference that excludes or down-weights that view reduces how much its own pose error the volume absorbs during large-error capture.

Learned methods are useful only as proposers. Preuhs et al.'s learned artefact-appearance objective reached a residual reprojection error of 0.013 mm. They also warn that data consistency "is not guaranteed" with learned components ([arXiv:2006.10390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10390) **[older]**). Thies et al. took reprojection error from 3 mm to 0.61 mm with a learned quality target ([arXiv:2401.09283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09283)). MissAlignment learns a self-supervised alignment score for cryo-ET ([bioRxiv 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.29.721716)). *Inference:* for a scientific library, these belong in initialisation or multi-start candidate generation. Acceptance should stay with data-consistency residuals, which TomoJAX already computes in `validation_residuals.py`.

### Laminography alignment is largely unpublished territory

Published laminography alignment is mostly instrument-side. LamNI reaches its 61° laminography angle with laser interferometry, giving positioning errors below 5 nm ([J. Synchrotron Rad. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7206541/) **[older]**). Kang et al.'s LamNI integrated-circuit study treats projection-matching alignment as a solved preprocessing step before learned missing-cone recovery ([arXiv:2304.04597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04597)). The APS transmission X-ray microscope (TXM) nano-laminography work aligns mechanically in two stages. Its authors found it "impractical" to evaluate pitch, roll and rotation-axis artefacts simultaneously. They reconstruct after manual axis and pitch searches and name "reliability and automation of sample alignment" as the open need ([J. Synchrotron Rad. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12591070/)). Sun et al. rank the sensitivity of cone-beam laminography geometric parameters from projection trajectories ([J. X-ray Sci. Technol. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3233/xst-221338)), the closest thing to a published identifiability analysis.

I found no arXiv paper that reports per-view three-rotation recovery in laminography or analyses how the missing cone limits each DOF. *Inference:* TomoJAX's 0.22° laminography result, still descending linearly at outer 64 and barely changed by the exact projector (0.246 → 0.219), is convergence-limited. The coupled step should help it most. Estimating global axis tilt and orientation as a separate low-dimensional parameter before freeing per-view rotations follows cryo-ET's tilt-axis practice ([AreTomo3, Nature Methods 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03093-y)). The numerical Schur spectrum should define which laminography modes are gauge and which are merely weak.

## Exact projectors and preconditioned Krylov make the coupled step affordable

The coupled step costs, in the internal review's estimate, 20–40 extra forward/adjoint pairs per outer. That is affordable only if the volume Krylov solve converges fast and each operator call is lean. Three bodies of work bear on this.

### Reconstruction acceleration feeds directly into the Schur solve

Because LAP's projected operator inherits J_x's preconditioners ([arXiv:1705.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09992)), every advance in preconditioning plain reconstruction also speeds up the coupled pose step. Sidky et al. used a ramp-filtered backprojector as the B in BA-GMRES. On real cone-beam CT (CBCT) data with 180 views this reached minimum image RMSE **at iteration 4 instead of 29**, and 4 iterations beat FBP (0.0210 vs 0.0347). Semi-convergence then arrives so quickly that iteration count gives only coarse control ([arXiv:2201.07408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07408)). Kumar and Donatelli write R*R as a multilevel Toeplitz operator, so each gradient costs two FFTs on a 2×-padded grid. A three-level hierarchy cut runtime about 5× on real nano-CT, and ramp-filtered initialisation saved 20–25 iterations ([arXiv:2603.28756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28756)). Myers et al.'s 3×3×3 volume-space deconvolution preconditioner inside full multigrid gave acceptable images after 1–2 fine iterations ([arXiv:2103.10329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10329) **[older]**). Sabaté Landman et al. measured Krylov methods in TIGRE at **35 s for 30 iterations** against SIRT at 3 min 50 s and OS-SART at 6 min 30 s at comparable quality ([arXiv:2211.14212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14212)).

*Inference:* for parallel-beam laminography with a full rotation, AᵀA is close to shift-invariant. Probing it once with a centred delta yields a Fourier preconditioner that is symmetric positive definite, regularised in the missing cone. That preconditioner fits CGLS/LSQR on the projected operator without changing Krylov method. No laminography-specific preconditioning paper exists, and preconditioning speeds convergence to the missing-cone plateau without lowering it. Krylov recycling across sequences of slowly changing systems ([arXiv:2309.15759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15759); [arXiv:2306.15049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15049)) is the Krylov analogue of FISTA warm-starting. *Inference:* it could carry the volume subspace across outer pose updates.

Because TomoJAX keeps its forward and adjoint exactly matched, CGLS/LSQR theory holds. The unmatched-pair machinery is needed only if a filtered B is introduced: AB/BA-GMRES ([arXiv:2110.01481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01481) **[older]**), hybrid ABBA with automatic Tikhonov ([arXiv:2602.17892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17892)), or GKB variants ([arXiv:2606.31153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31153)).

For the inner nonnegative Huber-TV FISTA, IML FISTA adds coarse corrections through the Moreau envelope of the non-smooth term and keeps convergence guarantees ([arXiv:2304.13329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13329)). *Inference:* TomoJAX's multiresolution pyramid already supplies the coarse operators. Vanmaele et al. show multilevel optimisation with box constraints beating L-BFGS far from the solution on ASTRA parallel-beam tomography ([arXiv:2505.11104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11104)). Randomised Nyström preconditioners speed FISTA-like solvers but cost K extra images and were tested only in 2D ([arXiv:2411.08178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08178)). Stochastic methods show large early gains: ProxSVRGSkip ran 5–20× faster, but on CPU in 2D ([arXiv:2602.09527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09527)). FB-LISA gave the best 3D walnut reconstruction at 120 s on a small GPU ([arXiv:2605.12085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12085)). Because TomoJAX's Huber-TV is smooth, the "skip the expensive prox" gain mostly does not apply. *Inference:* the view-subset gradients map naturally onto per-view poses.

For warm starts, Fathi, Skorikov and van Leeuwen learn FBP filters for 45° cone-beam laminography. Learned FBP gave MSE 0.094 against 0.160 for FDK, and on a real scan it "closely match[es]" a Nesterov-accelerated iterative solution ([arXiv:2602.13048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13048)). Beckmann and Nickel give closed-form MSE-optimal FBP filters needing no training ([arXiv:2408.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06471)).

### Pose derivatives should be fused, analytic and tape-free

Most differentiable CT libraries differentiate only with respect to the volume, where the gradient is just the adjoint. Examples are LEAP ([arXiv:2307.05801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05801)), CTorch ([arXiv:2503.16741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16741)), PYRO-NN ([arXiv:2511.08427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08427)) and TIGRE v3 ([arXiv:2412.10129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10129)). DiffDRR obtains pose gradients by autodiff through a tensorised Siddon tracer ([arXiv:2208.12737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12737)). Jiang et al. measured it at about 7.5× the memory and 7.8× the time of analytic gradients ([arXiv:2508.13304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13304)). *Inference:* in TomoJAX's fused pose-normal kernel, the rigid-pose derivative of one ray is a residual-weighted sum of three projected gradient components plus three position-weighted moments. All six can accumulate in the same ray pass as the forward projection. This feeds the exact per-view 5×5 pose blocks needed by the LAP elimination without storing columns.

FaCT-GS shows where kernel time goes. Its largest speed-up came from replacing a per-pixel backward pass with a per-primitive one, plus fused loss kernels ([arXiv:2604.01844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01844)). If gradients are ever taken through a reconstruction, as in a recon-layer objective, implicit differentiation gives memory independent of iteration count ([arXiv:2105.15183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15183) **[older]**). On precision, TomocuPy uses FP16 storage for fast reconstruction ([arXiv:2209.08450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08450)). FP32 matched FP64 with SSIM 0.99998 in QR-based CT ([arXiv:2412.07631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07631)). Half-precision inner products overflowed in iterative tomography ([arXiv:2210.03844](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03844); ID from a search snippet only). Pose Jacobians at the 0.01° level are a case for FP32 accumulation, and possibly higher-precision reductions in the pose normal equations.

### Compile overhead is a configuration problem first

No paper quantifies cold-start compilation for a JAX/Pallas tomography code. Phaser reports only a 6× per-iteration gain ([arXiv:2505.14372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14372)). The documentation is still actionable. JAX's persistent cache writes entries only when compilation exceeds `jax_persistent_cache_min_compile_time_secs`, which defaults to 1.0 s. It can also persist GPU autotune and kernel caches ([JAX docs](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/persistent_compilation_cache.html)). `jax.export` removes tracing but "still require[s] XLA compilation" ([JAX export docs](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/export/export.html)). `serialize_executable` skips both tracing and compilation for a fixed shape and device, but only from trusted bytes ([JAX API docs](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/jax.experimental.serialize_executable.html)). The official guide on slow compilation names the usual trace-cache defeaters: closures recreated in loops, unrolled Python loops and shape variability ([JAX docs](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/debugging/slow_tracing_compilation.html)). *Inference:* TomoJAX's roughly 7.5 s first outer, against 0.61 s per subsequent outer, is likely to fall sharply from three changes. The first is to lower the cache threshold to zero, because many per-kernel compiles fall below 1 s and are never cached. The second is to bucket volume and detector shapes across multiresolution levels and pass poses as traced arguments. The third is to make sure the new coupled solver is a single `lax.while_loop` program, not Python-level iteration.

## Conclusion

The evidence turns "add a Schur-complement step" into a specific design. Run alternation for capture. Then, near the solution, take a trust-region-damped pose step that eliminates the 305 pose parameters exactly and leaves the volume increment undamped. Its Krylov solve on the projected volume operator should use a tightening tolerance and keep the Huber-TV Hessian. Project the rigid-object gauge subspace out of every step, then do the FISTA re-solve TomoJAX already has. The in-progress coupled solver departs from this in two ways the literature flags as decisive: it requires positive volume damping, and it iterates on the stacked system with only a diagonal pose preconditioner. The new insight is that this step and the reconstruction-speed work are the same problem. Every preconditioner, multilevel scheme or Fourier/Toeplitz trick that speeds laminography CGLS also speeds the pose step, because LAP's projected operator inherits A's preconditioners.

The coupled step is necessary but probably not sufficient for the parallel cell. Its plateau has the fingerprint van Leeuwen et al. attribute to weakly identifiable low-frequency tomographic-angle errors. It also coincides with a finite-difference FP32 Jacobian resolving sub-0.01 px motions. The Schur eigenspectrum on a small problem and a discretisation-exact analytic pose Jacobian will separate these causes without relaxing any gate. They also lay groundwork for the ±3° capture range, where 61-view angular spacing sits exactly at the published identifiability edge. Two results would be new to the literature: a 3D exact-basis projector with analytic rigid-pose derivatives, and a Schur-complement per-view pose solver demonstrated on tilted geometry. Published laminography practice still aligns by hand.
